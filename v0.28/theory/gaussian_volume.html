Heterogeneities of any shape ​

Before this page

Concentration and contribution tensors for the tensors a scheme needs from a heterogeneity; Two-inclusion interaction tensors, §1, for the Lippmann-Schwinger equation and the Green operator ; Approximation by Gaussians for the generating function , the step and the shape parameter .

Eshelby's result gives the concentration tensor of an ellipsoid in closed form because the strain inside it is uniform. In a heterogeneity of another shape (a torus, a concave pore, a particle read from a tomography) the strain varies from point to point and has to be computed. This page solves the integral equation of a heterogeneity by the Gaussian approximation, an approach developed by Kanaun and coworkers (in conduction, in elasticity). The equation is written here for the polarization, which gives a single symmetric system for every contrast.

4. Accuracy ​

The Gaussians of the nodes next to the surface spread the polarization across it, and the depolarizing field is reduced in a layer of the thickness of a few steps. The error this leaves is larger than for a crack, and not as regular: it is what limits the method. Against Eshelby's solution for a sphere in conduction:

julia
K₀ = TensISO{3}(2.0)
relative_error(A, Aref) = norm(get_array(A) - get_array(Aref)) / norm(get_array(Aref))
ns = (8, 12, 16, 24)
plt = plot(; xscale = :log10, yscale = :log10, xlabel = "nodes across the sphere, n",
    ylabel = "relative error on the concentration tensor", legend = :bottomleft)
for (label, k₁) in (("ten times more conducting", 20.0), ("ten times less", 0.2)), extrapolate in (false, true)
    K₁ = TensISO{3}(k₁)
    exact = gradient_gradient_concentration(Ellipsoid(1.0), K₁, K₀)
    errors = [relative_error(gradient_gradient_concentration(GaussianInclusion(Ellipsoid(1.0); n, extrapolate), K₁, K₀), exact) for n in ns]
    plot!(plt, collect(ns), errors; marker = :circle, label = label * (extrapolate ? ", two grids" : ", one grid"))
end
plt

With the default grid of 24 nodes the error is 1 to 3 % for contrasts between and , on spheres and on ellipsoids of aspect ratio down to 0.4, in conduction and in elasticity. It grows with the contrast on the stiff side, where the fields inside the heterogeneity are less and less determined (they are not unique in a rigid body), and so does the number of iterations: beyond a contrast of the solve is slow, and a contrast of already gives the rigid limit within the error of the method.

5. Scope ​

  • The reference medium is isotropic; the heterogeneity may be anisotropic. A phase is registered in a scheme with symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize() when its reference would not be.

  • Derivatives with respect to the properties of the phases go through the solve by the implicit-function theorem, as for cracks, §8. A derivative with respect to the shape is refused.
