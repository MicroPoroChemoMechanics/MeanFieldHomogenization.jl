Hill tensors by reduction to a sphere ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/97_hill_reduction.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/97_hill_reduction.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

The tutorial Hill polarization tensors in practice, where hill_tensor is called directly on each geometry and its numerical back-ends are introduced, and the section Reduction to a sphere of the theory page on Hill tensors, where the relation used below is stated.

The Hill tensor of an ellipsoid in a matrix is that of a sphere in the stretched medium    , carried back by the same tensor    (Barthélémy, 2020):

When the stretched medium belongs to a class whose spherical Hill tensor has a closed form, the reduction is a closed form for the ellipsoid too. This page computes three Hill tensors that way and holds each to the three numerical back-ends of the package, applied to the ellipsoid itself: the residue algorithm (:residues), the adaptive cubature DECUHR (:decuhr) and nested Gauss-Kronrod quadratures (:nestedquadgk). Everything is written in tensor algebra: ⊠ˢ for the symmetrized box product, ⊡ for the double contraction, inv of a tensor, and no component is read by index.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization
using TensND
using LinearAlgebra
using Printf
import DECUHR, Integrals          # the `:decuhr` back-end lives in an extension

The reduction, and the numerical back-ends to hold it to ​

shape_tensor gives the symmetric     of an ellipsoid, its semi-axes and their orientation, from which the stretch follows. How the Hill tensor of the unit sphere in is computed is passed as a function, since that is where each example takes its closed form.

julia
stretch(ell) = (Ainv = inv(shape_tensor(ell)); Ainv ⊠ˢ Ainv)

function hill_by_reduction(ell, C, sphere_hill)
    B = stretch(ell)
    return B ⊡ sphere_hill(B ⊡ C ⊡ B) ⊡ B
end

relgap(P, Q) = norm(P - Q) / norm(Q)

const TOL = (; abstol = 1.0e-12, reltol = 1.0e-10)

function against_numerics(P_reduced, ell, C)
    for method in (:residues, :decuhr, :nestedquadgk)
        P = hill_tensor(ell, C; method, TOL...)
        @printf "  reduction against %-13s %.1e\n" method relgap(P_reduced, P)
    end
    return nothing
end

1. A spheroid in a coaxial transversely isotropic matrix ​

The matrix of Sevostianov et al. (2005), eq. 2.34, also that of Figure 1 of Barthélémy (2020), and an oblate spheroid of aspect ratio   sharing its axis .

julia
const e₃ = (0.0, 0.0, 1.0)
C_ti = TensND.ti_from_constants(2.179, 0.579, 0.689, 10.345, 1.0, e₃)
ω = 0.2
spheroid = Spheroid(ω)
B = stretch(spheroid)

The stretch keeps the class: is transversely isotropic about the same axis, its Walpole coefficients being those of divided by to the number of indices equal to 3 in the components they gather, (Barthélémy, 2020; eq. 19). Its best transversely isotropic fit is exact, and the ratio of the coefficients shows the powers of :

julia
C̃ = best_fit_ti(B ⊡ C_ti ⊡ B, e₃)
@printf "‖𝔹:ℂ:𝔹 − its TI fit‖ / ‖𝔹:ℂ:𝔹‖ = %.1e\n" relgap(C̃, B ⊡ C_ti ⊡ B)
collect(TensND.get_data(C̃) ./ TensND.get_data(C_ti)) .* [ω^4, 1, ω^2, 1, ω^2]
5-element Vector{Float64}:

The sphere in a transversely isotropic medium has a closed form, the case   of the coaxial spheroid; carried back, it is the Hill tensor of the spheroid, with no quadrature.

julia
P_reduced = hill_by_reduction(spheroid, C_ti, C̃ -> hill_tensor(Ellipsoid(1.0), best_fit_ti(C̃, e₃)))
against_numerics(P_reduced, spheroid, C_ti)
  reduction against residues      7.1e-16
  reduction against decuhr        9.3e-15
  reduction against nestedquadgk  2.8e-16

2. An elliptically orthotropic matrix, from the isotropic closed form ​

The elliptically orthotropic matrix ``\mathbb{C} = \lambda,\boldsymbol{D}\otimes\boldsymbol

  • 2\mu,\boldsymbol{D}\stackrel{s}{\boxtimes}\boldsymbol{D}``, with

symmetric positive definite, is mapped by the stretch onto the isotropic      , and the ellipsoid onto    (Barthélémy, 2020; eq. 14):

With coaxial with the ellipsoid, is the ellipsoid of semi-axes , whose Hill tensor in an isotropic matrix is a closed form in elliptic integrals: the matrix is orthotropic, and its Hill tensor is a closed form all the same.

julia
λ, μ = 2.0, 1.5
d = (1.6, 1.0, 0.7)
D = TensND.Tens(Diagonal(collect(d)))
C_eo = λ * (D ⊗ D) + 2μ * (D ⊠ˢ D)
semi_axes = (2.0, 1.0, 0.5)
ellipsoid = Ellipsoid(semi_axes...)

Dmh = TensND.Tens(Diagonal(collect(1 ./ sqrt.(d))))
Bd = Dmh ⊠ˢ Dmh
C_iso = TensISO{3}(3λ + 2μ, 2μ)                 # λ 𝟏⊗𝟏 + 2μ 𝟏⊠ˢ𝟏
P_reduced = Bd ⊡ hill_tensor(Ellipsoid((semi_axes ./ sqrt.(d))...), C_iso) ⊡ Bd
against_numerics(P_reduced, ellipsoid, C_eo)
  reduction against residues      1.8e-15
  reduction against decuhr        5.0e-15
  reduction against nestedquadgk  4.0e-16

3. A rotated triaxial ellipsoid in a triclinic matrix ​

With no class left to keep, a triclinic matrix and a rotated ellipsoid of three distinct semi-axes, the stretched medium is triclinic as well and the sphere in it needs a numerical method too. The reduction still pays: on the sphere the denominator   of the form of the Hill tensor is one. Here the sphere is treated by the residue algorithm.

julia
M = [
    210.0 80.0 75.0 5.0 4.0 3.0;
    80.0 195.0 90.0 -2.0 3.0 -1.0;
    75.0 90.0 220.0 1.0 -2.0 2.0;
    5.0 -2.0 1.0 60.0 2.5 1.5;
    4.0 3.0 -2.0 2.5 65.0 -1.0;
    3.0 -1.0 2.0 1.5 -1.0 55.0
]
C_tri = TensND.from_mandel(M, CanonicalBasis{3, Float64}())
rotated = Ellipsoid(2.0, 1.0, 0.5; euler_angles = (0.3, 0.8, -0.5))

P_reduced = hill_by_reduction(rotated, C_tri, C̃ -> hill_tensor(Ellipsoid(1.0), C̃; method = :residues, TOL...))
against_numerics(P_reduced, rotated, C_tri)
  reduction against residues      2.5e-15
  reduction against decuhr        1.3e-12
  reduction against nestedquadgk  1.3e-15

The three back-ends agree with the reduction to the tolerance asked for, and in the first two examples the reduction needs no quadrature at all.
