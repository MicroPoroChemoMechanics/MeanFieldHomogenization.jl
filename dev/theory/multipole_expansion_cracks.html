The multipole expansion method for penny-shaped cracks ​

Before this page

The multipole expansion method for spheroids and for spheroids in elasticity, whose harmonics, solutions, translations and linear systems are kept here.

A penny-shaped crack of radius and normal enters the expansion as a spheroid does, through its response to the harmonics it receives and the field it sends (Kushch, 2013). It is not taken as the limit of a flat spheroid: it is the focal disk of the oblate coordinates of focal distance , on which the spheroidal harmonics are finite, and its two lips are told apart by those coordinates.

using MeanFieldHomogenization, TensND, LinearAlgebra
a, R = 0.7, 2.0
asm = ParticleAssembly(; boundary = MixedBC(R))
add_matrix!(asm, Dict(:K => TensISO{3}(1.0)))
add_particle!(asm, :c, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), EllipticCrack(a, a), Dict{Symbol, Any}())
σ = -8a^3 / (3 * (4π * R^3 / 3))
canonical_components(homogenize(asm, MultipoleExpansion(; nmax = 3), :K))[3, 3] - (1 + σ / (1 - σ / 3))

3. A traction-free crack ​

In elasticity the crack sends the solutions of the method for spheroids in elasticity, built on the decaying harmonics of focal distance , and its lips are free of traction,    at   and  . A point of a lip has the same Cartesian coordinates as the facing point of the other lip; the solutions are therefore evaluated as functions of the oblate coordinates, smooth on the disk, and their Cartesian gradients follow from

the Jacobian of the chart being regular on the lips off the axis and the edge. The response is the least-squares solution of the traction-free condition at the Gauss points in of one meridian, both lips together.

The crack reads a close neighbor through the traction on its plane. That reading has a null space: the rigid motions, the uniform stresses in the plane of the crack and other regular solutions carry no traction there. It is immaterial: the response of the crack is a function of the traction alone,    , so any received coefficients with the traction read give the same sent field. The coefficients are solved for on a basis of the solutions that carry a traction, chosen by a QR factorization with column pivoting. One crack in a ball is again Maxwell's estimate, against the cracked medium of the package:

julia
C₀ = TensISO{3}(3 * 5 / 3, 2 * 1.0)
asm = ParticleAssembly(; boundary = MixedBC(R))
add_matrix!(asm, Dict(:C => C₀))
add_particle!(asm, :c, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), EllipticCrack(a, a), Dict{Symbol, Any}())
rve = RVE(; distribution_shape = Ellipsoid(1.0))
add_phase!(rve, :M, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :F, PennyCrack(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); density = a^3 / (4π * R^3 / 3))
km(C) = mandel(C, CanonicalBasis{3, Float64}())
norm(km(homogenize(asm, MultipoleExpansion(; nmax = 2), :C)) - km(homogenize(rve, Maxwell()))) / norm(km(C₀))
2.220446049250313e-16
