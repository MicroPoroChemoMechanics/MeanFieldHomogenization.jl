The multipole expansion method for spheroids in elasticity ​

Before this page

The multipole expansion method in elasticity, whose solutions, translation, linear system and stiffness are kept here, and the method for spheroids in conduction, for the spheroidal harmonics.

The particles are now spheroids of revolution axis , all oriented alike, bonded perfectly or by springs, or empty, in an isotropic matrix of shear modulus and Poisson ratio (Kushch, 2013). Each one keeps the unknowns of a sphere, the coefficients of the regular spherical solutions it receives about its center; what changes is its response, which goes through solutions adapted to its shape, and the reading of the field of a close neighbor.

using MeanFieldHomogenization, TensND, LinearAlgebra
iso(κ, μ) = TensISO{3}(3κ, 2μ)
stiffness(asm, scheme) = TensND.mandel(homogenize(asm, scheme, :C), TensND.CanonicalBasis{3, Float64}())
asm = ParticleAssembly(; boundary = MixedBC(Ellipsoid(2.0, 2.0, 0.4)))
add_matrix!(asm, Dict(:C => iso(1.0, 0.6)))
add_particle!(asm, :p, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), Ellipsoid(1.0, 1.0, 0.2), Dict(:C => iso(10.0, 6.0)))
norm(stiffness(asm, MultipoleExpansion(; nmax = 2)) - stiffness(asm, MoriTanaka())) / norm(stiffness(asm, MoriTanaka()))

3. From one spheroid to another ​

Outside the sphere that holds its focal segment or disk, the field a spheroid sends is a series of the decaying spherical solutions ; its coefficients are fitted by least squares, order by order, on a sphere of twice its largest semi-axis, up to the degree  . The response is then written as the matrix that turns the received spherical coefficients into the sent spherical ones, and the linear system, the translation, the periodic sums and the stiffness of the method in elasticity apply unchanged. As in conduction, the series of a sender converges about a receiver only if their distance exceeds the sum of their focal distances; below 2.5 times that sum the field of the sender is evaluated at points of the surface of the receiver and projected there, by least squares, on the regular spherical solutions, which holds at any distance between particles that do not overlap. On a periodic cell the images within that distance are treated so and left out of the lattice sums, and the decaying solutions of high degree are summed directly, as in conduction.
