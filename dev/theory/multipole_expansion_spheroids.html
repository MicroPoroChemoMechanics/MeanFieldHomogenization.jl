The multipole expansion method for spheroids ​

Before this page

The multipole expansion method for spheres, whose structure is kept here, and Layered spheroid for the confocal coordinates, the transfer matrices and the imperfect interfaces of a spheroid.

The particles of the assembly are now spheroids of revolution axis , all oriented alike, possibly layered and with imperfect interfaces, in an isotropic matrix of conductivity ; they may differ in size, aspect ratio and conductivity. The method is that of the spheres (Kushch, 2013): about each particle the field is a series of harmonics, the series of one particle is rewritten about the others, and the coefficients solve a linear system. Three things change: the harmonics are spheroidal, the response of a particle couples the degrees, and the translation from one particle to another cannot always go through the spherical harmonics. The scheme is the same MultipoleExpansion; an assembly of spheroids selects this page, an assembly of spheres the previous one.

4. Periodic arrays ​

In a periodic assembly, held in a rectangular cell of periods (PeriodicBox((L₁, L₂, L₃))), each particle stands for the lattice of its images, the images of particle included. The images of within the threshold of particle are translated one by one by the near form; the others enter the far form through the sums of   over them.

Forming these sums as the sums over the whole lattice minus the near images fails: the near images contribute terms larger than the result by a factor that grows as with the degree, being the distance of the closest one, and the difference loses its digits as the degree grows. The sums are formed in two ways instead. Up to the degree 8, where those of the lowest degrees converge only conditionally, Ewald's splitting of the previous page runs with the near images excluded: their short-range terms are skipped and the long-range part they carry,   , is taken off, the splitting parameter being lowered so that this part stays small. Above the degree 8 the sums converge absolutely and are formed directly over the images up to eight times the threshold. On the lattice of section 6, both are accurate to about of the sums.

The background of the lattice adds, as for spheres, the uniform gradient   per spherical dipole of the lattice (section 5 below).
