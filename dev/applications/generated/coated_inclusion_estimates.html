Coated inclusions: closures on the mean strains, and the exact solution ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/74_coated_inclusion_estimates.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/74_coated_inclusion_estimates.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Layered inclusions, which gives the exact solutions used here, and Custom inclusions, through which the estimates of this page enter the schemes.

An inclusion surrounded by coatings can be treated in two ways. The first keeps one unknown per layer, its mean strain, and closes the problem by an assumption on the field of the coatings: it applies to any ellipsoid and costs a linear system. The second solves the field in each layer, which is what the layered inclusions of the package do, for the shapes where that field is known. This page writes two closures of the first kind, the multi-inclusion model of Hori and Nemat-Nasser and the formulation of Dinzart, Sabar and Berbenni, with which that of Lipinski, Barhdadi and Cherkaoui coincides, states the assumption of each, and measures what it costs against the exact solution: on composites of coated spheres, with the parameters of the second article, then in conduction on confocal spheroids (Bonfoh et al., 2020).

# Matrix of a property: Kelvin-Mandel at order 4, components at order 2.
km(t::TensND.AbstractTens{4}) = SMatrix{6, 6}(TensND.mandel(t, CanonicalBasis{3, eltype(t)}()))
km(t::TensND.AbstractTens{2}) = SMatrix{3, 3}(TensND.canonical_components(t))
hill(shape, C) = km(hill_tensor(shape, C))

abstract type Closure end
struct MultiInclusion <: Closure end        ## Hori and Nemat-Nasser (1993)
struct Revisited <: Closure end             ## Dinzart, Sabar and Berbenni (2016)
struct Interfacial <: Closure end           ## Lipinski, Barhdadi and Cherkaoui (2006)

# Row `J` of the system, as the 6×6 blocks that multiply each mean strain, and
# the factor of the remote strain on its right-hand side.
function relation(::MultiInclusion, J, shapes, C, C₀, φ, ω)
    P = [hill(s, C₀) for s in shapes]
    blocks = map(eachindex(C)) do K
        ΔC = km(C[K]) - km(C₀)
        K ≤ J ? (φ[K] / ω[J]) * (I + P[J] * ΔC) : (P[K] - P[K - 1]) * ΔC
    end
    return blocks, 1.0
end
# The tensor of a coating, from the Hill tensors of its two boundaries in its own medium.
interaction(::Revisited, outer, inner, ω_outer, ω_inner) = outer - (ω_outer / (ω_outer - ω_inner)) * (outer - inner)
interaction(::Interfacial, outer, inner, ω_outer, ω_inner) = inner - (ω_inner / (ω_outer - ω_inner)) * (outer - inner)
function relation(closure::Union{Revisited, Interfacial}, J, shapes, C, C₀, φ, ω)
    T = interaction(closure, hill(shapes[J + 1], C[J + 1]), hill(shapes[J], C[J + 1]), ω[J + 1], ω[J])
    blocks = map(eachindex(C)) do K
        K ≤ J ? φ[K] * ((1 / ω[J + 1] - 1 / ω[J]) * I - (φ[J + 1] / (ω[J] * ω[J + 1])) * T * (km(C[K]) - km(C[J + 1]))) :
            K == J + 1 ? (φ[K] / ω[J + 1]) * one(T) : zero(T)
    end
    return blocks, 0.0
end
# The exact relation on the whole inclusion.
function whole(shapes, C, C₀, φ)
    P = hill(last(shapes), C₀)
    return [φ[K] * (I + P * (km(C[K]) - km(C₀))) for K in eachindex(C)], 1.0
end

"""
    mean_strains(closure, shapes, C, C₀) -> (matrices, fractions)

Mean strain of each layer per unit remote strain, as a matrix, and the volume
fractions of the layers in the inclusion: `shapes[J]` is the ellipsoid of the
layers 1 to `J`, `C[K]` the property of the layer `K`, `C₀` that of the
surrounding medium.
"""
function mean_strains(closure::Closure, shapes, C, C₀)
    N, n = length(C), size(km(C₀), 1)
    volume(s) = prod(s.semi_axes)
    ω = [volume(s) / volume(last(shapes)) for s in shapes]
    φ = [ω[1]; diff(ω)]
    rows = [[relation(closure, J, shapes, C, C₀, φ, ω) for J in 1:(N - 1)]; [whole(shapes, C, C₀, φ)]]
    A = reduce(vcat, [reduce(hcat, blocks) for (blocks, _) in rows])
    b = reduce(vcat, [factor * Matrix(1.0I, n, n) for (_, factor) in rows])
    X = A \ b
    return [X[(n * (K - 1) + 1):(n * K), :] for K in 1:N], φ
end

The estimate enters the schemes as a custom inclusion, by its two concentration tensors: the mean strain and the mean stress of the whole inclusion per unit remote strain.

julia
function coated_estimate(closure, shapes, C)
    strain(_, C₀; kw...) = (((ε, φ) = mean_strains(closure, shapes, C, C₀)); TensND.from_mandel(sum(φ .* ε)))
    stress(_, C₀; kw...) = (((ε, φ) = mean_strains(closure, shapes, C, C₀)); TensND.from_mandel(sum(φ[K] * km(C[K]) * ε[K] for K in eachindex(C))))
    return CustomInclusion(
        last(shapes).semi_axes; homogeneous = false,
        gradient_gradient_concentration = strain, flux_gradient_concentration = stress,
    )
end

The generalized self-consistent scheme ​

The examples of (Dinzart et al., 2016) are composites of spheres, whose exact reference is the generalized self-consistent scheme: the composite inclusion is the particle, its coatings and a shell of matrix, in the proportions of the material, embedded in the effective medium. With the layered sphere of the package this is the self-consistent scheme on a cell of one phase; with an estimate it is the same scheme on the custom inclusion. The parameters are those of section 4 of the article: all the phases have the Poisson ratio 0.3, and the moduli are divided by the shear modulus of the matrix.

julia
const ν = 0.3
stiffness(μ) = iso_stiffness(2μ * (1 + ν) / (3 * (1 - 2ν)), μ)
radii(fractions) = cbrt.(cumsum(fractions))                 ## of concentric spheres, the outer one of radius 1

# The iteration starts from the matrix: an estimate has no layers to average.
function effective(inclusion, C_matrix)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :composite, inclusion, Dict(:C => C_matrix); fraction = 1.0)
    return isotropify(homogenize(rve, SelfConsistent(; init = C_matrix), :C))
end
effective_shear(inclusion, C_matrix) = shear_modulus(effective(inclusion, C_matrix))
effective_bulk(inclusion, C_matrix) = bulk_modulus(effective(inclusion, C_matrix))
exact(μs, fractions) = effective_shear(LayeredSphere(Tuple(radii(fractions)), Tuple(stiffness.(μs))), stiffness(last(μs)))
estimate(closure, μs, fractions) =
    effective_shear(coated_estimate(closure, [Ellipsoid(r) for r in radii(fractions)], stiffness.(μs)), stiffness(last(μs)))

The field of a coating ​

Both closures describe a coating by one tensor. In the exact solution of concentric spheres the displacement of each layer under a remote shear is a combination of four radial functions, in , , and , whose amplitudes follow from the continuity of the displacement and of the stress vector at the interfaces (Layered sphere): the strain of a coating varies with the radius and with the direction. It is drawn here for a sphere of contrast 6 in a coating ten times softer than the matrix, of thickness 0.2 times its radius, alone in the matrix under the remote shear : the ratio along the axis , in the plane of the shear, and along , normal to it.

A test with a known answer: the core behind a soft coating ​

A core surrounded by a coating whose stiffness tends to zero cannot be loaded: the stress vector vanishes on its boundary, hence its mean stress, whatever the shape and the moduli. Two consequences can be read without any reference computation: the mean stress of the core tends to zero with the stiffness of the coating, and the effective moduli no longer depend on the core, the material being a porous one whose pores have the size of the coated particle.

The first is tested on the coated sphere alone in the matrix, by the ratio of the mean shear stress of the core to the remote one,    :

Conduction: confocal spheroids ​

In conduction the fields of confocal ellipsoidal layers are known exactly, and (Bonfoh et al., 2020) show that a closure of this kind is then exact. The two closures above are applied to the conductivities and set beside the confocal spheroid of the package. The contrasts are those of section 5.1 of that article,   and  , for a prolate core of aspect ratio 5 whose coating is a tenth of its volume; the inclusion is alone in the matrix, and the numbers are the transverse and the axial components of its mean gradient per unit remote gradient.

julia
let aspect = 5.0, focal² = aspect^2 - 1                        ## core of semi-axes (1, 1, 5)
    # The confocal spheroid whose volume is 1.1 times that of the core, by bisection on its transverse semi-axis.
    volume(b) = b^2 * sqrt(b^2 + focal²)
    lower, upper = 1.0, 2.0
    for _ in 1:60
        middle = (lower + upper) / 2
        volume(middle) < 1.1 * volume(1.0) ? (lower = middle) : (upper = middle)
    end
    disks = (1.0, lower)
    axes = sqrt.(disks .^ 2 .+ focal²)
    K, K₀ = [TensISO{3}(100.0), TensISO{3}(10.0)], TensISO{3}(1.0)
    exact_gradient = km(gradient_gradient_concentration(LayeredSpheroid(axes, disks, Tuple(K); Nseries = 8), K₀, K₀))
    @printf "%-16s  transverse %.6f   axial %.6f\n" "exact" exact_gradient[1, 1] exact_gradient[3, 3]
    shapes = [Ellipsoid(d, d, a) for (d, a) in zip(disks, axes)]
    for (name, closure) in ("multi-inclusion" => MultiInclusion(), "revisited" => Revisited())
        ε, φ = mean_strains(closure, shapes, K, K₀)
        A = sum(φ .* ε)
        @printf "%-16s  transverse %.6f   axial %.6f\n" name A[1, 1] A[3, 3]
    end
end

multi-inclusion   transverse 0.036582   axial 0.172753
revisited         transverse 0.030576   axial 0.158466

The revisited closure coincides with the exact solution here; in elasticity the field of an ellipsoidal coating is not uniform even when the layers are confocal, and no closure on the mean strains is exact.

What follows for the package ​

A closure on the mean strains is exact where its assumption holds (the bulk modulus of concentric spheres and the conduction of confocal ellipsoids for the second one) and otherwise carries a deviation that grows with the contrast and with the volume fraction, with no bound known beforehand. The package therefore solves the field of the layers wherever it is known: concentric spheres and coaxial cylinders (LayeredSphere, LayeredCylinder), with imperfect interfaces, and confocal spheroids (LayeredSpheroid); for layers of any other shape it computes a finite-element cell (Finite-element inclusions). The closures of this page are not part of it: they are written here in a few lines on its Hill tensors, as any closure of the kind can be, and entered through the custom-inclusion interface.
