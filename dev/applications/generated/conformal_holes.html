Before this page

Holes given by a conformal map, which derives the tensors used here, and Conformal holes, which gives the syntax.

The pores of a ceramic or of a rock are seldom elliptic in section. This page takes prismatic pores whose section is a hypotrochoid or a polygon, compares their compressibility and their shear compliance with the values published by Ekneligoda and Zimmerman for maps of two to four terms, carries the polygons to the limit of the map, and ends with the stiffness and the conductivity of a matrix that contains such pores, a perforated plate included.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization
using TensND
using LinearAlgebra
using JSON
using Printf
using Plots

The pores and the two numbers compared ​

The section of the pore is the image of the unit circle by    . A single term gives a hypotrochoid, with   rounded corners; the map of a regular polygon, cut after a few terms, gives a polygon with rounded corners (polygon_map).

julia
contour(hole) = [ζ + sum(m * ζ^(-k) for (k, m) in pairs(hole.coefficients)) for ζ in cis.(range(0, 2π; length = 721))]
function draw(holes; kw...)
    panels = [
        plot(
            real.(contour(h)), imag.(contour(h)); aspect_ratio = 1, fill = (0, 0.35, :orange), color = :black,
            legend = false, axis = false, grid = false, title = name, titlefontsize = 8
        ) for (name, h) in holes
    ]
    return plot(panels...; layout = (1, length(holes)), size = (170 * length(holes), 190), kw...)
end

The matrix is isotropic, of shear modulus and Poisson ratio , in plane strain in the plane of the section. Two numbers measure the pore:

  • its compressibility, the relative change of its area under a unit hydrostatic stress in the plane;

  • its shear compliance, its shear strain, in the engineering sense, under a unit shear stress in the plane.

Both are proportional to   (Holes given by a conformal map, section 4), and both equal 2 for the circle once divided by this factor. They are read on the compliance contribution tensor of the pore, whose axis is :

julia
const K₀, μ₀ = 1.7, 0.6                                  ## bulk and shear moduli of the matrix
const ν₀ = (3K₀ - 2μ₀) / (2 * (3K₀ + μ₀))
const C₀ = iso_stiffness(K₀, μ₀)
const void = 0 * C₀
compliance(hole) = canonical_components(inverse_property_contribution(hole, void, C₀))
function compressibility(hole)
    H = compliance(hole)
    Σ = (2ν₀, 1.0, 1.0)                                  ## hydrostatic in the plane; the axial stress is the reaction of plane strain
    return sum(H[i, i, j, j] * Σ[j] for i in 2:3, j in 1:3) * μ₀ / (1 - ν₀)
end
function shear_compliance(hole)                          ## averaged over the directions of the shear in the plane
    H = compliance(hole)
    return μ₀ * (H[2, 3, 2, 3] + (H[2, 2, 2, 2] - 2H[2, 2, 3, 3] + H[3, 3, 3, 3]) / 4) / (1 - ν₀)
end
compressibility(ConformalHole(())), shear_compliance(ConformalHole(()))      ## the circle
(2.0, 2.0000000000000004)

A pore with a threefold axis is isotropic in its plane and the average does nothing; the square is not, and the published values are averaged.

The shear compliance against the direction of the shear ​

A section with a fourfold axis is not isotropic in its plane: its shear compliance depends on the angle between the shear and the pore. For the hypotrochoid   the article finds (its equation (5.5)) the mean value above multiplied by  . Turning the pore by is turning the shear by ; a coefficient of a pore turned by is  .

julia
turned(hole, α) = ConformalHole(Tuple(m * cis((k + 1) * α) for (k, m) in pairs(hole.coefficients)))
pure_shear(hole) = 2 * μ₀ * compliance(hole)[2, 3, 2, 3] / (1 - ν₀)
m₃ = 0.2
square_like = ConformalHole(3 => m₃)
θs = range(0, π; length = 73)
plot(
    θs, [pure_shear(turned(square_like, -θ)) for θ in θs]; lw = 2, label = "computed",
    xlabel = "angle θ of the shear", ylabel = "normalized shear compliance", size = (600, 340),
    xticks = ([0, π / 4, π / 2, 3π / 4, π], ["0", "π/4", "π/2", "3π/4", "π"]), title = "Hypotrochoid n = 3, m = $m₃", titlefontsize = 10,
)
plot!(θs, [2 * (1 - m₃ * cos(4θ)) / ((1 - m₃^2) * (1 - 3 * m₃^2)) for θ in θs]; ls = :dash, lw = 2, label = "equation (5.5) of the article")

A scaling law on the perimeter ​

The same article proposes to estimate the shear compliance of a pore from its perimeter and its area alone, by   (its equation (6.1)), exact for the circle. The perimeter of the image of the unit circle is the integral of :

julia
perimeter(hole; n = 4096) = 2π / n * sum(abs(1 - sum(k * m * ζ^(-k - 1) for (k, m) in pairs(hole.coefficients))) for ζ in cis.(2π .* (0:(n - 1)) ./ n))
area(hole) = π * (1 - sum(k * abs2(m) for (k, m) in pairs(hole.coefficients)))
scaling_law(hole) = 1 + perimeter(hole)^2 / (4π * area(hole))
println("                                scaling law: published, computed   exact shear compliance")
for row in [published_2008["two_terms"]; published_2008["three_terms"]]
    @printf "%s   %8.4f   %8.4f            %8.4f\n" name(row) row["scaling_law"] scaling_law(pore(row)) shear_compliance(pore(row))
end

A matrix with prismatic pores ​

Aligned pores of the same section, at the porosity , by the scheme of Mori and Tanaka. The stiffness is compared through two moduli of the plane of the sections: the plane-strain bulk modulus    and the shear modulus averaged over the directions of the plane.

julia
function plane_moduli(hole, f)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :pores, hole, Dict(:C => void); fraction = f)
    C = canonical_components(homogenize(rve, MoriTanaka(), :C))
    return (; k = (C[2, 2, 2, 2] + C[2, 2, 3, 3]) / 2, μ = (C[2, 3, 2, 3] + (C[2, 2, 2, 2] - C[2, 2, 3, 3]) / 2) / 2)
end
sections = ["circle" => ConformalHole(()), "hexagon" => polygon(6, 100), "square" => polygon(4, 100), "triangle" => polygon(3, 100)]
fs = range(0, 0.3; length = 16)
k_matrix, μ_matrix = K₀ + μ₀ / 3, μ₀
bulk = plot(; xlabel = "porosity f", ylabel = "k / k₀", title = "plane-strain bulk modulus", titlefontsize = 9, legend = :bottomleft)
shear = plot(; xlabel = "porosity f", ylabel = "μ / μ₀", title = "shear modulus in the plane", titlefontsize = 9, legend = false)
for (name, hole) in sections
    moduli = [plane_moduli(hole, f) for f in fs]
    plot!(bulk, fs, [x.k / k_matrix for x in moduli]; lw = 2, label = name)
    plot!(shear, fs, [x.μ / μ_matrix for x in moduli]; lw = 2)
end
plot(bulk, shear; layout = (1, 2), size = (760, 340), left_margin = 4Plots.mm, bottom_margin = 4Plots.mm)

The fewer the sides, the softer the porous material: at equal porosity a triangular section lowers the plane bulk modulus markedly more than a circular one, and a hexagonal section is hardly told from it.

The schemes whose reference is the estimate ​

The scheme of Mori and Tanaka solves each pore in the matrix. The self-consistent and the differential schemes solve it in their own estimate, which aligned pores make transversely isotropic about their axis when the section has an axis of symmetry of order 3, or of order 5 and more. Such a medium is isotropic in the plane of the section, and the pore is solved in it as in an isotropic one (Holes given by a conformal map, section 7). The medium is here seen in its plane: the cell holds the stiffness of the plane of the matrix, a tensor of dimension 2, and the pores as their sections (Plane problems).

julia
const disk = Ellipsoid(1.0; dim = 2)
function plane_cell(c, hole, f; kw...)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, disk, Dict(:C => c); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :pores, hole, Dict(:C => 0 * c); fraction = f, kw...)
    return rve
end
const c_strain = plane_strain_stiffness(C₀)
schemes = ["Mori-Tanaka" => MoriTanaka(), "differential" => DifferentialScheme(), "self-consistent" => SelfConsistent()]
fs = range(0, 0.25; length = 11)
plane_bulk(t) = (c = canonical_components(t); (c[1, 1, 1, 1] + c[1, 1, 2, 2]) / 2)
panels = map(["circle" => ConformalHole(()), "triangle" => polygon(3, 100)]) do (name, hole)
    panel = plot(; xlabel = "porosity f", ylabel = "k / k₀", title = name, titlefontsize = 9, legend = name == "circle" ? :bottomleft : false, ylims = (0, 1))
    for (label, scheme) in schemes
        plot!(panel, fs, [plane_bulk(homogenize(plane_cell(c_strain, hole, f), scheme, :C)) / k_matrix for f in fs]; lw = 2, label)
    end
    panel
end
plot(panels...; layout = (1, 2), size = (760, 340), left_margin = 4Plots.mm, bottom_margin = 4Plots.mm)

The three schemes share their tangent at the origin, which the compressibility of the pore fixes, and part as the porosity grows; the self-consistent estimate of the triangular pores vanishes at a porosity below that of the circular ones.

A square section has a fourfold axis only, and aligned square pores leave the plane anisotropic. Oriented at random in the plane, by the average declared on the phase, they give an isotropic plane again:

julia
at_random = plane_cell(c_strain, polygon(4, 100), 0.1; symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize())
bulk_shear(isotropify(homogenize(at_random, SelfConsistent(), :C))) ./ (k_matrix, μ_matrix)
(0.5716777834062754, 0.6904576042539842)

A perforated plate ​

A thin plate loaded in its plane is in plane stress. Its mean fields satisfy the equations of plane strain with another bulk modulus, and the cell is the same with the plane-stress stiffness of the plate. Young's modulus of a plate with holes of four sections, by the self-consistent scheme. The estimate is a general tensor of the plane, which these sections leave isotropic: its moduli are read on its isotropic part.

julia
const c_stress = plane_stress_stiffness(C₀)
plate_modulus(cell) = first(plane_strain_to_plane_stress(young_poisson(isotropify(homogenize(cell, SelfConsistent(), :C)))...))
E₀ = young_modulus(C₀)
shapes = [
    "circle" => f -> plane_cell(c_stress, ConformalHole(()), f), "hexagon" => f -> plane_cell(c_stress, polygon(6, 100), f),
    "square, at random" => f -> plane_cell(c_stress, polygon(4, 100), f; symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize()),
    "triangle" => f -> plane_cell(c_stress, polygon(3, 100), f),
]
plate = plot(; xlabel = "porosity f", ylabel = "E / E₀", title = "Perforated plate, self-consistent scheme", titlefontsize = 10, legend = :topright, size = (600, 340))
for (name, cell) in shapes
    plot!(plate, fs, [plate_modulus(cell(f)) / E₀ for f in fs]; lw = 2, label = name)
end

Conduction ​

In conduction the answer is one number. A regular polygon has no term , so that the gradient concentration tensor of an insulating prismatic pore is isotropic in its plane,   : twice the ratio of the areas of the unit disk and of the section.
