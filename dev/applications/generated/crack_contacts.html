Cracks with contacts between their faces ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/75_crack_contact_islands.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/75_crack_contact_islands.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

The manual page Planar cracks of any contour, and the theory page of the same name, §5 and §6, for the compliance–resistance connection and for contacts.

The faces of a real crack often touch on small areas where they stay bonded. Such contacts stiffen the crack far more than their area suggests, and a solid whose cracks have contacts behaves as if its crack density were lower. This page follows the study of Markov, Kanaun and Kachanov of a circular crack of radius with circular contacts of radius : one contact, centered then shifted; many contacts on a regular pattern; contacts gathered in a cluster; contacts placed at random. Each configuration is one region, the disk without the contacts.

The curves of the paper are published as images, from which values cannot be read back with precision. The comparisons below are with what the paper states in words and numbers, and, for a centered contact, with the annular crack of the package, computed by an independent method.

The crack, and what measures the effect of its contacts ​

A crack with contacts is the disk without the disks of the contacts. n is the number of nodes across the crack; a contact needs about three nodes on its radius to be seen, and the faces about six steps of the grid to open between two contacts (below).

julia
contacts(centers, l) = reduce(∪, [Disk(l; center = c) for c in centers])
crack_with(centers, l; n = 128) = PlanarCrack(setdiff(Disk(1.0), contacts(centers, l)); size = 1.0, n)
free = PlanarCrack(Disk(1.0); n = 128)
PlanarCrack{Float64} (Ellipse, size ℓ=1.0, 12732 nodes, h=0.015625)

The effect of the contacts is measured by the reduction factor of the normal compliance,  , the superscript 0 standing for the crack without contact. Since the contribution of a crack to the compliance of a solid is proportional to , a crack with contacts counts as a free crack of radius  .

In an isotropic matrix, the normal opening per unit traction and the jump of potential per unit flux solve the same equation up to a constant (theory, §5), so that    for any contour (Kachanov and Argatov, 2023): the reduction factor of the resistance is too. The check on a crack with three contacts:

julia
three = crack_with([(0.4, 0.1), (-0.3, 0.35), (-0.1, -0.5)], 0.12)
Bnn = get_array(cod_tensor(three, C₀))[3, 3]
b = cod_tensor(three, K₀)
Bnn / b, k₀ * (1 - ν) / μ
(1.8199999999999978, 1.82)

is therefore computed below from the conduction problem, which has one unknown per node where elasticity has three.

julia
const b₀ = cod_tensor(free, K₀)
λ(crack) = cod_tensor(crack, K₀) / b₀

One centered contact ​

The crack is then a ring, the AnnularCrack of the package, whose opening is computed by a Galerkin method on Chebyshev polynomials (Contributions from intensity factors). The two methods share nothing but the integral equation.

julia
radii = 0.1:0.1:0.8
λ_gauss = [λ(crack_with([(0.0, 0.0)], l)) for l in radii]
λ_ring = [cod_tensor(AnnularCrack(1.0, l), K₀) / cod_tensor(PennyCrack(1.0), K₀) for l in radii]
maximum(abs.(λ_gauss .- λ_ring))
0.0006606682668518493
julia
plt = plot(

One shifted contact ​

A contact at the distance of the center makes the crack anisotropic in its plane: the three principal values of (sliding along the shift, sliding across it, opening) are reduced by three factors  , with  .

julia
B⁰ = diag(get_array(cod_tensor(free, C₀)))
shifted = [(0.2, 0.5), (0.4, 0.4), (0.6, 0.3), (0.8, 0.15)]
β = [diag(get_array(cod_tensor(crack_with([(c, 0.0)], l; n = 96), C₀))) ./ B⁰ for (l, c) in shifted]
for ((l, c), βᵢ) in zip(shifted, β)
    @printf(
        "l/a = %.1f, c/a = %.2f:  β = (%.3f, %.3f, %.3f)   largest gap %.1f %%\n",
        l, c, βᵢ..., 100 * (maximum(βᵢ) - minimum(βᵢ)) / βᵢ[3]
    )
end

The three factors stay close: the paper reports gaps below 6 % even for large contacts, and below 3 % for  . The reduction of the compliance by a contact is nearly the same in opening and in sliding, which is what allows a single factor to be used for both.

Many contacts on a regular pattern ​

contacts on a hexagonal lattice of step :   for zero to four rings around the central one. The step is taken as    for rings, which spreads the contacts over the crack (the paper does not give its value).

julia
hexagon(k, d) = [(d * (i + j / 2), d * j * sqrt(3) / 2) for i in (-k):k for j in (-k):k if abs(i + j) ≤ k]
patterns = [(k, 1 + 3k * (k + 1), 2 / (2k + 1)) for k in 0:4]

panels = map(patterns) do (k, N, d)
    region = setdiff(Disk(1.0), contacts(hexagon(k, d), 0.3 * d))
    heatmap(
        coverage(region, GaussianGrid(region, 100))'; aspect_ratio = :equal, colorbar = false,
        axis = false, color = :grays, title = "N = $N", titlefontsize = 9
    )
end
plot(panels...; layout = (1, 5), size = (780, 180))

What a grid has to resolve is the opening between the contacts rather than the contacts. On a dense lattice every opening is narrow, which is why the grid of the curves above follows the radius of the contacts. In a cluster or in a random draw, a narrow opening is the exception, between two contacts that happen to be close, and carries little of the opening of the crack.

Holm's radius. The radius of the single centered contact that stiffens the crack as much as the cluster. It is found on the annular crack, by bisection.

julia
ring_λ(c) = cod_tensor(AnnularCrack(1.0, c), K₀) / cod_tensor(PennyCrack(1.0), K₀)
function holm_radius(target)
    lo, hi = 1.0e-3, 0.99                       ## λ decreases from 1 to 0 with the radius
    for _ in 1:40
        mid = (lo + hi) / 2
        ring_λ(mid) > target ? (lo = mid) : (hi = mid)
    end
    return (lo + hi) / 2
end
holm = holm_radius(first(λ_cluster))

Contacts placed at random ​

contacts of equal radius, at the density  , drawn at random without overlap; six draws for each . A draw that cannot be completed is started again.

julia
function random_contacts(rng, N, l; gap = 0.02)
    centers = Tuple{Float64, Float64}[]
    rejected = 0
    while length(centers) < N
        ρ, θ = (1 - l - gap) * sqrt(rand(rng)), 2π * rand(rng)
        c = (ρ * cos(θ), ρ * sin(θ))
        if all(q -> hypot(c[1] - q[1], c[2] - q[2]) > 2l + gap, centers)
            push!(centers, c)
        elseif (rejected += 1) > 10_000                 ## jammed: start again
            empty!(centers)
            rejected = 0
        end
    end
    return centers
end

rng = MersenneTwister(2025)
numbers = (5, 10, 20)

function young_modulus(crack, ε)
    rve = RVE(; distribution_shape = Ellipsoid(1.0))
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :cracks, crack, Dict(:C => C₀); density = ε, symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize())
