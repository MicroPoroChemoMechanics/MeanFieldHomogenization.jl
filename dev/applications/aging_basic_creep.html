Aging basic creep of concrete: a CEM I cement, with and without silica fume ​

Before this page

A hydrating blended cement paste, coupled to its chemistry for the four-scale elastic model and the hydration it is fed with, and Aging creep of solidifying cementitious materials for a phase that solidifies. The nesting of aging scales is in the manual.

Lavergne and Barthélémy (2020) turn the elastic model of Lavergne et al. (2018) into an estimate of the basic creep of concrete: the creep of a sealed specimen, with no drying. Two things are added to the elastic chain:

  • the hydrates creep, logarithmically, as nanoindentation suggests;

  • the microstructure changes under load: hydrates keep forming, grains keep dissolving, and a phase formed after the loading carries none of it.

This page follows the same idea with the hydration computed by ChemistryLab, and holds it against the creep tests of Le Roy (1995) on two concretes of one cement, loaded from 0.83 to 28 days.

The creep of the hydrates ​

A hydrate creeps with a logarithmic compliance whose Poisson ratio does not change in time:

with half a day. For C-S-H, the ratio follows a quadratic fit in the Ca/Si ratio, divided by the relative humidity (Lavergne and Barthélémy, 2020; Eq. (11)): the lower the humidity, the stiffer the creep. AFm takes the ratio of a C-S-H of Ca/Si 1.7; every other hydrate is elastic.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization
using TensND
using JSON
using Printf
using Plots
gr()  # headless backend; GKSwstype is set to "100" in make.jl

const SCRIPTS = joinpath(pkgdir(MeanFieldHomogenization), "scripts")
include(joinpath(SCRIPTS, "common", "paste_micromechanics.jl"))
include(joinpath(SCRIPTS, "common", "aging_creep_micromechanics.jl"))

E, ν = PASTE_E_NU["C-S-H"]
dt = exp10.(range(-2, 3; length = 120))
plot(; xscale = :log10, xlabel = "t - t' [days]", ylabel = "E J(t, t')", legend = :topleft,
    size = (700, 380), title = "Creep of C-S-H, Ca/Si = $(CSH_CA_SI)")
for rh in (1.0, 0.75, 0.5)
    r = csh_log_ratio(CSH_CA_SI, rh)
    plot!(dt, 1 .+ log1p.(dt ./ CREEP_TAU) ./ r; lw = 2, label = @sprintf("RH = %.2f, E_D/E = %.2f", rh, r))
end
current()

Phases that form and dissolve ​

In aging viscoelasticity a phase keeps one volume fraction over the whole history. What changes is when it carries stress, and two operations of the package say it:

  • precipitated(law, t_s): a phase formed stress-free at , insensitive to any strain applied before;

  • dissolved(law, t_d): a phase that stops carrying stress at .

The hydration is cut into steps. Each step adds phases to the scales of the elastic model:

scaleschemephases
hydratesself-consistent, spheresthe hydrates formed during one step: one law per step
hydrate foamself-consistent, needles of aspect ratio 7, random orientationsthe foam born at step : the hydrates precipitated at each later step, and the pores left
pasteMori–Tanakathe foam born first as matrix; a grain consumed at step is dissolved at while the foam born at is precipitated in its place
concreteMori–Tanakathe paste and elastic aggregates

Each scale is a homogenize_alv on one time grid, and hands its result, a VolterraOperator, to the next as a phase property; the paste enters the concrete as a Homogenized cell. Every operator here is isotropic: two   matrices instead of one  . The elastic phases (the unhydrated grains, the aggregates) are given by their stiffness tensor itself, whose kernel is   . The model is scripts/common/aging_creep_micromechanics.jl; a phase consumed and replaced, for instance, is one line of it, the sum of two operators:

julia
R = dissolved(grain, times[i]) + precipitated(foams[i], times[i])

A small check of these two operations: a soft phase in which a stiff one precipitates at one day, loaded at three days, has the compliance of the stiff phase.

julia
times = [0.5, 1.0, 2.997, 3.0, 30.0]
C = iso_stiffness_E_nu(30.0, 0.2)
soft = VolterraOperator(TensISO{3}(3.0e-6, 2.0e-6), times)
stiffening = soft + precipitated(VolterraOperator(C, times), 1.0)
J = uniaxial_compliance(stiffening, times)
@printf "J(30, 3) = %.5f per GPa, 1/E = %.5f\n" J[end, 4] 1 / 30
J(30, 3) = 0.03333 per GPa, 1/E = 0.03333

On the grid, a load "held from " rises over . The grid therefore carries an instant just before each loading age (loading_grid), so that this rise is a step.

Le Roy's concretes ​

The cement is the Saint-Vigor Portland cement of Le Roy (1995), whose phase composition is given by Lavergne and Barthélémy (2020) (Table 4), with the effective water-to-cement ratios they derive. Two concretes were loaded at several ages:

julia
paper = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(MeanFieldHomogenization), "data", "literature", "lavergne2020.json"))["quantities"]
for p in ("leroy_ordinary", "leroy_hpc")
    @printf "%-15s w/c = %.3f  a/c = %.2f  cement = %3.0f kg/m3  silica fume = %2.0f %%\n" p paper[p * "_w_c"]["value"] paper[p * "_a_c"]["value"] paper[p * "_cement"]["value"] get(paper, p * "_silica_fume", Dict("value" => 0))["value"]
end
leroy_ordinary  w/c = 0.463  a/c = 5.46  cement = 342 kg/m3  silica fume =  0 %
leroy_hpc       w/c = 0.298  a/c = 4.73  cement = 398 kg/m3  silica fume = 10 %

The whole chain — hydration, four aging scales, compliance for every loading age — runs in scripts/47_aging_basic_creep_concrete.jl, about eight minutes; this page reads its results. The measurements are those of the NU database of Hubler et al. (2015), entries c_123_01 to c_123_10.

julia
res = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(MeanFieldHomogenization), "docs", "src", "assets", "applications", "aging_basic_creep", "results.json"))
tests = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(MeanFieldHomogenization), "data", "literature", "leroy1995.json"))["tables"]["basic_creep"]
panels = map(res["concretes"]) do conc
    p = plot(; xscale = :log10, xlabel = "t [days]", ylabel = "J(t, t') [1e-6/MPa]",
        ylims = (0, 130), legend = :topleft, title = conc["name"], titlefontsize = 9)
    for (k, cv) in enumerate(conc["curves"])
        test = only(filter(t -> t["nu_file"] in conc["files"] && t["loading_age_days"] == cv["age"], tests))
        rows = test["creep_compliance"]["rows"]
        plot!(p, Float64.(cv["t"]), Float64.(cv["J"]); lw = 2, color = k, label = "t' = $(cv["age"]) d")
        scatter!(p, cv["age"] .+ first.(rows), last.(rows); color = k, ms = 2.5, msw = 0, label = "")
    end
    p
end
plot(panels...; layout = (1, 2), size = (900, 420), left_margin = 3Plots.mm, bottom_margin = 4Plots.mm)

Lines: the model; points: the measurements.

What it shows ​

  • For the ordinary concrete loaded at 3 days or later, the model follows the shape of the creep curves, the elastic part included, and overestimates the compliance by 6 to 33 %, the most at 3 days.

  • Loaded at 0.83 and 1.12 days, it overestimates the compliance by far (the 0.83-day curve of the ordinary concrete is off the scale): the paste is then close to its percolation threshold, where the self-consistent foam is most sensitive. Lavergne and Barthélémy (2020) observe the same before 3 days.

  • For the high-performance concrete, the late creep is overestimated. One cause not modeled here: at so low a water-to-cement ratio the paste dries itself as it hydrates, which lowers its internal humidity and, through the of the creep ratio, its creep; the humidity is taken as 1 throughout.

What is not here ​

  • The C-S-H keeps the Ca/Si of the hydration model's single C-S-H (1.667), even where the silica fume lowers it.

  • The hydrates formed at a step take the composition of what increased during that step. Species that dissolve later (ettringite into monosulfate, portlandite consumed by the silica fume) are not taken out of older hydrates; the paper does this last in, first out.

  • The needles of the foam are averaged over random orientations, where the elastic model of Lavergne et al. (2018) uses 20 orientation bins: the aging self-consistent scheme needs an isotropic reference.

  • The paper's proposed correction, rescaling the estimate by the elastic compliance measured at loading, and its drying-creep model are not applied.
