Levin's theorem: free strains and prestresses ​

Before this page

Homogenization schemes, where the strain and stress concentration tensors of an estimate are defined, and any of the scheme pages of this chapter, each of which ends with the concentration tensors of its estimate.

The schemes of this chapter estimate the response of a composite to a macroscopic strain alone. The phases of a real material often carry, in addition, a strain that owes nothing to the stress — a thermal dilation, a hydric or chemical swelling — or a stress that owes nothing to the strain, such as the pressure of a fluid filling the pores. The macroscopic law then acquires an additional term, and the result known as Levin's theorem states that this term follows from the concentration tensors already computed, without any new problem to solve. Its consequences for thermal expansion were drawn by Rosen and Hashin (1970) for two anisotropic phases, the relations between the eigenstrains of the phases and their concentration tensors are developed for multiphase media by Dvorak and Benveniste (1992), and the theorem is the basis of the micromechanics of saturated porous media (Dormieux et al., 2006).

7. In the package ​

concentration_tensors returns the concentration tensors of every phase under a scheme, and the theorem is applied by levin_preflux and levin_free_gradient, which take the prestress or the eigenstrain of each loaded phase:

julia
ct = concentration_tensors(rve, MoriTanaka(:M), :C)
𝟏 = TensISO{3}(1.0)
α_hom = levin_free_gradient(ct, Dict(:M => α_M * 𝟏, :I => α_I * 𝟏))
B = -levin_preflux(ct, Dict(:P => -𝟏, :cracks => -𝟏))

A crack family is accepted as a prestressed phase, with its density as weight, and refused as an eigenstrained one, since it holds no material. The same two functions serve conduction: they take vectors and the concentration tensors of a conduction estimate. In elasticity they also answer to levin_free_strain and levin_prestress, which refuse a conduction estimate:

julia
ct_K = concentration_tensors(rve, MoriTanaka(:M), :K)
G = levin_free_gradient(ct_K, Dict(:M => g_M, :I => g_I))   # g_M = Tens([0.0, 0.0, 1.0]), …
