Damage by the growth of microcracks ​

In most damage models, a scalar variable degrades the stiffness through a function chosen to fit tests. This page derives a damage law from the microstructure instead. The variables are the densities of the families of microcracks that the solid contains, the stiffness is the homogenized stiffness of the cracked solid, and the evolution follows from an energy balance on the cracks. The approach is that of Dormieux and Kondo (2016), applied by Dormieux et al. (2006) to fluid-saturated media. The example runs the law.

Stability ​

Under a fixed strain, a family on its criterion keeps growing if a small increase of its density raises the driving force more than the resistance, that is, if    at fixed strain. The growth is then unstable. With Griffith's resistance is negative: once the criterion is reached, the density jumps to a larger value, the first one where the driving force has fallen back to the resistance, and the stress drops. A resistance that increases fast enough makes the growth stable and the damage progressive.

The law in MaterialPoints ​

CrackGrowthDamage builds a ResidualLaw whose internal variables are the densities. Over a step from it solves, for every family, one of the two following equations:

The first is the complementarity of the growth and of the criterion: its root either keeps the density or puts the family on its criterion. Both terms are dimensionless, so that they compare. A step is elastic, and the densities are kept, when every family is below its criterion at the densities of the last step.

The consistent tangent comes, as for any law of the chapter, from the implicit-function theorem applied to these equations. It contains the softening due to the growth of the cracks during the step. A density must stay positive; a Newton correction that leads to a negative density is halved until it does not.

Scope ​

crackspenny-shaped, open, with traction-free faces: no unilateral contact, no friction
familiesany number, oriented or isotropically distributed; each its own resistance
schemesany linear scheme of the package that handles cracks
softeninga softening law localizes in a structure; the response of a finite-element computation then depends on the mesh, which a point computation does not show
