Nonlinear poroelasticity of a cracked rock ​

A rock that contains microcracks is soft under a small confinement and stiffens as the confinement grows, because its cracks close one after the other. The same mechanism makes its Biot coefficient decrease with the confinement: a closed crack no longer stores fluid, and no longer transmits the pore pressure to the solid. Walsh (1965) explained the nonlinear compressibility of rocks in this way. Deudé et al. (2002) put it in a micromechanical setting of nonlinear poroelasticity. This page derives the law that MeanFieldHomogenization builds from the linear poroelasticity of the cracked solid. The example runs it.

What closes the cracks ​

The loading of the saturated rock is the macroscopic stress and the pore pressure . It splits into two problems. In the first, the pore pressure acts everywhere: on the faces of the cracks and in the pores, and, through a stress  , on the solid. This field is uniform; it carries no concentration at the tips of the cracks and cannot open or close them. The second problem is that of the dry rock under the Terzaghi effective stress

and that problem alone opens and closes the cracks. This argument, which the fractured-rock material of Barthélémy and Daniel (2011) already rests on, holds whatever the stiffness of the solid. It is the effective stress of the closure. The law is hypoelastic: the moduli are tangent moduli, and in general they are not the second derivatives of a potential. The crack compliance mixes normal and shear parts, while the closure depends on the normal traction only. The response is therefore obtained by integrating the rates along the loading path, not by a formula of the current state.
