Problems of the plane ​

Before this page

Conventions, whose conventions hold in the plane as in space. The tensors of dimension 2, their classes and their two pairs of elastic moduli are those of TensND: Tensors of the plane, of space and of any dimension.

A medium invariant along an axis, or a thin plate loaded in its plane, is homogenized on a cell of the plane: its inclusions are sections, its properties tensors of dimension 2, and the chain of the following pages holds with  . This page fixes what a tensor of the plane stands for, then lists where each problem of the plane is treated.

1. The cell of the plane ​

The axis of invariance, or the normal to the plate, is ; the plane is that of , whose vectors are the first and the second of the basis of the cell, with Greek indices. Two states reduce a material of space to the plane.

  • Plane strain,  : a body long along the axis. The stiffness of the plane is the block of on the plane, .

  • Plane stress,  : a thin plate, for the mean fields through its thickness. The three strains are condensed out: in Kelvin–Mandel form, the stiffness of the plane is the inverse of the block on the plane of the compliance . When the plane is a plane of symmetry of the material it reduces to   .

Both are tensors of the plane, of any anisotropy, and the problem of the plane does not depend on the state that produced it. Besides the tensor of no symmetry, a stiffness of the plane has three classes:

classconstants of a stiffnessinvariant under
isotropic, every rotation and reflection of the plane
square3the symmetries of a square, in a frame
orthotropic4, or 5 without the major symmetrythe reflections across the two axes of a frame
no symmetry6the half turn only

A rotation of the plane turns every direction of it, except the half turn, which leaves every tensor of even order unchanged: no rotation is left about a direction of the plane, and the plane has no transversely isotropic class.
