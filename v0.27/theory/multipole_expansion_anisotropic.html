The multipole expansion method with anisotropic constituents ​

Before this page

The multipole expansion method for spheroids and for ellipsoids, whose harmonics, projection and translations are used here unchanged.

The matrix and the particles may now be anisotropic in conduction. An affine change of variables makes an orthotropic matrix isotropic (Kushch et al., 2017); in the new variables the particles are still ellipsoids, of anisotropic conductivity, and the response of such a particle is obtained by projecting the interface conditions on its surface harmonics. Nothing else in the method changes.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization, TensND, LinearAlgebra
Q = [cos(0.4) -sin(0.4) 0; sin(0.4) cos(0.4) 0; 0 0 1]
K₁ = Tens(Q * Diagonal([5.0, 2.0, 8.0]) * transpose(Q))
asm = ParticleAssembly(; boundary = MixedBC(Ellipsoid(2.0, 2.0, 1.0)))
add_matrix!(asm, Dict(:K => TensISO{3}(1.0)))
add_particle!(asm, :p, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), Ellipsoid(1.0, 1.0, 0.5), Dict(:K => K₁))
mem = canonical_components(homogenize(asm, MultipoleExpansion(; nmax = 3), :K))
mt = canonical_components(homogenize(asm, MoriTanaka(), :K))
norm(mem - mt) / norm(mt)
1.9037749345105626e-16

3. Spheres of anisotropic conductivity ​

The solver of the method for spheres holds one scalar response per degree. A sphere of anisotropic conductivity, or a spheroid that the map of section 1 turns into a sphere, goes instead through the solver of shaped particles of the spheroids, its harmonics being the solid ones: the translation of the spheres holds at any distance between particles that do not overlap, and replaces the projection on the receiving surface.

4. Spheroids in a transversely isotropic matrix ​

Kushch and Sevostianov (2014) tabulate the anisotropy parameter   of spheroids of semi-axes and conductivity   in a matrix of conductivities across and along , on an orthorhombic lattice of periods proportional to the semi-axes. At  ,   and the fraction 0.3 they give 1.376:

julia
semi = (1.0, 1.0, 2.0)
cell = ParticleAssembly(; boundary = PeriodicBox(cbrt(4π / 3 / 0.3) .* semi))
add_matrix!(cell, Dict(:K => TensTI{2}(1.0, 0.5, (0.0, 0.0, 1.0))))
add_particle!(cell, :p, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), Ellipsoid(semi...), Dict(:K => TensISO{3}(10.0)))
k = diag(canonical_components(homogenize(cell, MultipoleExpansion(; nmax = 6), :K)))
round((k[3] / 0.5) / k[1]; digits = 3)
1.376
