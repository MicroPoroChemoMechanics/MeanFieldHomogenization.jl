Homogenizing nonlinear phases: secant methods ​

The laws of this chapter so far describe one material. This page turns to a composite whose phases are each described by such a law, and asks for the law of the composite. When the phases are linear, the schemes of the package answer exactly within their assumptions: the effective stiffness follows from the stiffnesses of the phases, their fractions and their shapes. When a phase is nonlinear, its stiffness changes from point to point with the local strain, and no scheme applies as it stands. The secant methods bring the problem back to a sequence of linear ones. This page derives them; the example runs them.

The phases ​

Each phase , of volume fraction , is a nonlinear elastic material with no internal variable: its stress depends on the current strain only. It is isotropic, so that its energy depends on the strain through two invariants only, the volume change and the equivalent strain  , being the deviator of . It needs no formula from the law.

This rewriting is exact, but it does not yet help: in a composite the strain varies inside each phase, so the secant stiffness varies too, and the phase is not a homogeneous linear material.

The classical secant method: the average strain ​

The simplest measure takes the equivalent strain of the average strain of the phase. A linear scheme gives that average through the strain concentration tensor of the phase,   , so that

This is ClassicalSecant. The average hides the fluctuations of the strain inside the phase: a strain that is large in some regions and small in others can average to a small value. In a matrix that wraps around stiff particles, for example, the strain concentrates near the particles, and the classical measure underestimates the strain that sets the secant modulus.

The modified secant method: the second moment ​

A measure that sees the fluctuations is the second moment of the equivalent strain over the phase,  . A linear scheme does not give second moments directly, but they follow from the energy of the comparison composite, as the following argument shows.

The modified method has a further standing. For phases whose energy is a concave function of , as for the power law with  , the variational procedure of Ponte Castañeda (1991) reaches the same equations. That procedure expresses the effective energy of the nonlinear composite through an optimization over linear comparison composites, and gives a bound when the linear estimate it uses is itself a bound. The review of Suquet (1997) sets out this connection and the other secant and variational estimates.

The composite as a law of MaterialPoints ​

The fixed point has one unknown per nonlinear phase. Written as a residual,

it has exactly the form of the local problem of a law: the effective strains play the role of the internal variables, the macroscopic strain that of the gradient, and the macroscopic stress that of the flux. NonlinearHomogenized builds that law. Everything the chapter says about a law then applies to the composite:

  • the fixed point is solved by Newton's method, with the Jacobian obtained by differentiating the scheme itself;

  • the consistent tangent of the composite, , comes from the implicit-function theorem. It contains the variation of the effective strains with , which a tangent read on the frozen comparison composite ( alone) would miss;

  • the composite can be driven along any path by point_response, and it can be used at the quadrature points of a finite-element computation.

Because the phases have no internal variable, the composite has no history either. The effective strains are recomputed at every step, the previous ones serving only as the starting point of the Newton iteration.

Scope ​

phasesisotropic, laws of the strain alone (nonlinear elasticity, deformation theory); a phase without a law keeps its linear stiffness
schemesany linear scheme of the package, with its own assumptions on the microstructure
effective strainone per nonlinear phase: the deviatoric response is secant, the bulk response is read at the same strain size
not coveredphases with internal variables (plasticity by increments, viscosity), for which the incremental and tangent variants of the secant idea are needed
