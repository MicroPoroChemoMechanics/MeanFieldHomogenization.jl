Writing a law ​

A law is written as plain functions and a NamedTuple of parameters. There are two ways to write one, and both give a material that runs everywhere in the package:

routeyou givesuits
PotentialLawa free energy, and a dissipation potential if the law has internal variableselasticity, viscoelasticity, any law of the generalized standard family
ResidualLawthe fluxes, and the residual of the local equations over a stepplasticity, damage, any law written as evolution equations

Neither asks for a derivative: the consistent tangent and the derivatives with respect to the parameters are obtained by automatic differentiation (see Integrating a law over a step).

What the functions receive ​

The functions of a law receive their arguments by name:

argumentwhat it isread as
gthe gradients at the end of the stepg.ε (strain), g.p (pore pressure), …
y (or α)the internal variables at the end of the stepy.εᵖ, y.d, …
prevthe state at the beginning of the stepprev.y.εᵖ, prev.g.ε
Δtthe length of the stepa number
pthe parameters of the lawp.E, p.ℂ, …

A symmetric tensor is a Mandel vector of six components; a scalar is a number. On Mandel vectors the double contraction is sum(a .* b), a fourth-order tensor is an SMatrix{6, 6} applied by *, and the helpers trace_mandel, deviator, von_mises and equivalent_strain give the usual invariants.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization, StaticArrays
ε = SVector(1e-3, -2e-4, -2e-4, 0, 0, 0)       # a Mandel vector
trace_mandel(ε), equivalent_strain(ε)
(0.0006000000000000001, 0.0008)

A first law by the potential route ​

Linear isotropic elasticity is the free energy     , being the deviator of the strain. It has no internal variable, so no dissipation:

julia
elastic = PotentialLaw(;
    name = :MyElasticity,
    energy = (g, α, p) -> p.k / 2 * trace_mandel(g.ε)^2 + p.μ * sum(abs2, deviator(g.ε)),
    p = (k = 160e3, μ = 80e3),                      # MPa
)
MyElasticity (potential route; ε → σ; no internal variable)

point_update integrates it over one step. It returns the stress, the tangent and the new state:

julia
σ, ℂ, state = point_update(elastic, ε, initial_state(elastic), 0.0)
σ
6-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{6, Float64} with indices SOneTo(6):
 224.00000000000006
  32.000000000000014
  32.0
   0.0
   0.0
   0.0

The tangent is the stiffness    , here in Mandel form: its [1, 2] entry is  .

julia
ℂ[1, 2], 160e3 - 2 * 80e3 / 3
(106666.66666666666, 106666.66666666666)

Adding an internal variable: a Maxwell solid ​

A spring in series with a dashpot carries a viscous strain . The free energy stores the elastic part, the dissipation potential the viscous rate. Both are written; the layout names the internal variable and its size:

julia
ℂ_mandel = 3 * 160e3 * (identity_mandel() * identity_mandel()' / 3) +
           2 * 80e3 * (one(SMatrix{6, 6, Float64}) - identity_mandel() * identity_mandel()' / 3)
maxwell = PotentialLaw(;
    name = :MyMaxwell,
    energy = (g, α, p) -> sum((g.ε - α.εᵛ) .* (p.ℂ * (g.ε - α.εᵛ))) / 2,
    dissipation = (α̇, α, p) -> p.τ * sum(α̇.εᵛ .* (p.ℂ * α̇.εᵛ)) / 2,
    layout = (εᵛ = 6,),
    p = (ℂ = ℂ_mandel, τ = 2.0),
)
MyMaxwell (potential route; ε → σ; internal variables εᵛ)

Over a step the viscous strain minimizes the incremental energy; the code solves that minimization and its tangent. Held at a fixed strain, the stress relaxes:

julia
r = point_response(maxwell, LoadingPath(range(0, 4; length = 41); ε11 = 1e-3, ε22 = 0, ε33 = 0))
r[:σ11][end] / r[:σ11][1], exp(-4 / 2.0)        # implicit scheme vs exact relaxation
(0.14204568230027795, 0.1353352832366127)

The two numbers differ by the error of the implicit scheme over steps of 0.1; finer steps bring them together. The same law ships as MaxwellViscoelasticity.

The residual route: plasticity and damage ​

When a law is written as evolution equations, give the residual whose root is the end of the step. Here is a scalar damage law: the stiffness is degraded by a factor  , and grows with the strain energy beyond a threshold , as   , never decreasing.

julia
Y(ε, p) = sum(ε .* (p.ℂ * ε)) / 2                    # strain energy of the undamaged material
d_of(ε, p) = 1 - sqrt(p.Y₀ / Y(ε, p))                 # the damage the threshold calls for

damage = ResidualLaw(;
    name = :MyDamage,
    stress = (ε, y, p) -> (1 - y.d) * (p.ℂ * ε),
    residual = (y, g, prev, Δt, p) -> (d = y.d - d_of(g.ε, p),),
    elastic = (g, prev, Δt, p) -> d_of(g.ε, p) <= prev.y.d,   # below the threshold: d frozen
    layout = (d = 1,),
    p = (ℂ = ℂ_mandel, Y₀ = 0.05),
)
MyDamage (residual route; ε → σ; internal variables d)

Three functions:

  • stress gives the stress from the strain and the internal variables (a law of the strain alone may give stress; a coupled law gives fluxes, below);

  • residual returns, by name, the equations the internal variables satisfy at the end of the step: here   ;

  • elastic says when the step leaves them unchanged: below the threshold, or when unloading. It is what makes the damage irreversible.

julia
path = LoadingPath(range(0, 1; length = 5); ε11 = t -> 1e-3 * sin(π * t), ε22 = 0, ε33 = 0)
rd = point_response(damage, path)
rd[:d]
5-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.1339745962155614
 0.38762756430420553
 0.38762756430420553
 0.38762756430420553

The damage grows while the strain rises, and stays at its maximum when it falls back: the elastic test kept it.

For plasticity the residual collects the flow rule and the consistency condition; J2Plasticity is written exactly this way, in about twenty lines of library.jl that can serve as a template.

Coupled fields ​

A law of several primal fields declares them, with their conjugate fluxes in the same order, and returns its fluxes by name. Biot's poroelasticity couples the strain and the pore pressure:

julia
b, M = 0.8, 1e4
biot = PotentialLaw(;
    name = :MyBiot,
    energy = (g, α, p) -> sum(g.ε .* (p.ℂ * g.ε)) / 2 - p.b * g.p * trace_mandel(g.ε) - g.p^2 / (2p.M),
    gradients = (ε = 6, p = 1), flux_names = (σ = 6, φ = 1),
    signs = (φ = -1,),                         # φ = −∂ψ/∂p
    p = (ℂ = ℂ_mandel, b = b, M = M),
)
F, 𝕂, _ = point_update(biot, SVector(1e-3, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2.0), initial_state(biot), 0.0)
𝕂[7, 7], 1 / M                                  # ∂φ/∂p = 1/M
(0.0001, 0.0001)

The tangent 𝕂 is the   matrix of all the derivatives: its blocks are , , and . A thermo-hydro-mechanical law adds the temperature and the entropy the same way, gradients = (ε = 6, p = 1, T = 1); a field of another size, a temperature gradient of size 3 with the heat flux, is declared by its size too. The residual route takes the same gradients and flux_names, with fluxes = (g, y, p) -> (σ = …, φ = …) in place of stress.

External variables: a temperature ​

Some variables are read by a law without being returned with a flux: a temperature imposed by a separate heat computation, a time, a degree of hydration. They are given by name for the step, external = (T = …,), and the law reads them in p, beside its parameters. A thermoelastic law, whose free expansion is    :

julia
thermal(g, p) = g.ε - p.α * (p.T - p.T₀) * identity_mandel()
thermo = PotentialLaw(;
    name = :Thermoelastic,
    energy = (g, a, p) -> sum(thermal(g, p) .* (p.ℂ * thermal(g, p))) / 2,
    p = (ℂ = ℂ_mandel, α = 1.2e-5, T₀ = 293.0),
)
σ_hot, _, _ = point_update(thermo, zero(ε), initial_state(thermo), 0.0; external = (T = 343.0,))
σ_hot[1], -3 * 160e3 * 1.2e-5 * 50            # blocked expansion: −3kαΔT
(-288.00000000000006, -288.0)

A loading path gives them as functions of time, and a value already in p takes the value given, which makes a parameter vary along the path:

julia
heating = LoadingPath(0:0.25:1; external = (T = t -> 293.0 + 100t,))   # free faces
point_response(thermo, heating)[:ε11]           # α ΔT along the ramp
5-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0003000000000000001
 0.0006
 0.0009
 0.0012000000000000001

Checking a law ​

check_material_interface exercises a law against the Gauss-point contract, including the tangent against a central finite difference of the stress (a check, not a computation the law ever uses):

julia
check_material_interface(damage; verbose = false), check_material_interface(biot; verbose = false)
(true, true)

Parameters belong in p

Put every number of the law in p: building the law again with ForwardDiff.Dual parameters then differentiates its whole response with respect to them (see Plugging a law into a computation).

Symbolic numbers

A law without internal variable evaluates with Symbolics.Num entries, which gives its stress and tangent in closed form, as the elastic law above does. One restriction comes from Symbolics itself: its ForwardDiff extension (version 7.47) fails on muladd with a symbolic number among dual ones, which a matrix product such as p.ℂ * g.ε performs; write such a law with traces and deviators, as above, until that is fixed. A law with internal variables is solved iteratively and refuses symbolic numbers with a clear message.
