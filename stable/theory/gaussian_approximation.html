Approximation by Gaussians ​

The closed forms of the previous pages hold for ellipsoids and for a few cracks. A crack of any contour, or a heterogeneity of any shape, is the solution of an integral equation that has to be solved numerically. MeanFieldHomogenization does it by expanding the unknown on Gaussians centered at the nodes of a regular grid, an approximation introduced by Maz'ya and Schmidt and developed in their monograph. This page presents the approximation alone, on functions of one variable; the next one applies it to cracks.

4. At the ends of the grid ​

The estimate (3) holds for nodes that fill the space (Maz'ya and Schmidt, 1996). On an interval, the Gaussians of the nodes that would lie beyond its ends are missing, and the first figure of this page shows the result. This section quantifies it.

Let the nodes fill the half-line  :   ,  . Replacing the sum over the nodes by an integral over the half-line, divided by , the sum of the generating functions is no longer one:

It is one half at the end of the grid and reaches one a few widths of a Gaussian further in. The first moment, which vanished in Section 3, no longer does:      . The expansion of around then gives

At the end of the grid is half of , whatever the function and whatever the step: the error there is of order one, and refining the grid does not reduce it. Only the layer where it occurs narrows, in proportion to . A polynomial shows it. Drawn against the distance to the end counted in steps, the ratio comes down to the curve (4) as the step decreases:

julia
using SpecialFunctions: erf

law(ξ) = (1 + erf(ξ / sqrt(2.0))) / 2                       # (4), with H = 2

ξs = range(0, 5; length = 200)                              # distance to the end, in steps

    plot!(plt, ξs, [ph((ξ / n,)) / p(ξ / n) for ξ in ξs]; lw = 1.5, label = "h = 1/$n")
end
plot!(plt, ξs, law.(ξs); ls = :dash, color = :black, label = "(4)")

The ratio tends to one half as the second term of (5) says, in proportion to , and (4) itself holds to about one percent. In dimension the sum of the Gaussians is a product of such factors: is half of on a face of the grid, and a quarter of it at a corner of a rectangular grid.

Three situations follow.

  • The function is known beyond the ends. The grid is extended by a few widths , as in the second figure of Section 1: is one over the whole interval and (3) holds again.

  • The function is known on the interval only. It has to be continued beyond the ends before it is sampled, and is worth near the ends what the continuation is worth.

  • The function is the unknown of an integral equation and vanishes at the edge of its domain, as the opening of a crack does. Nothing can be sampled outside, and the Gaussians of the last nodes spread the unknown beyond the edge, whose position is then known to within a step. The next page, §4, corrects it by a rule on the nodes of the edge.

5. Why Gaussians, for an integral equation ​
