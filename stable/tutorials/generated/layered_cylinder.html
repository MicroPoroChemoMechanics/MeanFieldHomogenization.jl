The whole page as one script

Before this page

The theory page Layered cylinder, which derives the solution used here, and the tutorial n-layer sphere: volume-averaged concentration tensors, its spherical counterpart.

A unidirectional composite of coated fibers, from one fiber to the effective medium and its creep. The fiber is transversely isotropic about its axis , as a carbon fiber is; a soft isotropic sizing surrounds it, bonded by a spring interface; the matrix is isotropic. All the moduli are in GPa and the lengths in µm.

The fibers are along , the default. Fibers of another direction are given by the axis keyword of LayeredCylinder, their transversely isotropic layers being then built about that same axis (Layered inclusions); every result below is turned with them and is otherwise unchanged.

2. Inside the fiber ​

Under a remote transverse shear      ,  , in the matrix, the stress on a section. LayeredCylinderFields solves the problem once; the fields are then evaluated point by point.

julia

The stress is uniform in the fiber, where the core modes are those of a homogeneous strain; it varies in the sizing and the matrix. Along the radius   the radial traction is continuous across the spring while the displacement jumps by :

julia
for side in (:inner, :outer)

    σ = canonical_components(local_flux(fields, a, 0.0, 0.0, E; side))
    @printf("%-6s u₁ = %.5f µm   σ₁₁ = %.3f MPa\n", side, u[1], 1.0e3 * σ[1, 1])
end

The pointwise field and the layer averages come from the same amplitudes, and they agree: the strain averaged over the sizing,    , is the one gradient_gradient_concentration gives for its layer. The integral over the annulus is computed here by quadrature. In the strain varies as and , which 32 equally spaced angles integrate exactly; in , 12 Gauss–Legendre points.

julia
strain(r, θ) = canonical_components(local_gradient(fields, r, θ, 0.0, E; layer = 2))
rs, ws = gauss(12, a, a + e)
θs = 2π .* (0:31) ./ 32
ε_quadrature = sum(ws[i] * rs[i] * strain(rs[i], θ) for i in eachindex(rs), θ in θs) *
    (2π / 32) / (π * ((a + e)^2 - a^2))
ε_layer = canonical_components(gradient_gradient_concentration(fiber, C_matrix; layer = 2) ⊡ E)
maximum(abs, ε_quadrature - ε_layer) / maximum(abs, ε_layer)

3. The aligned composite ​

Fibers at the volume fraction of carbon in the composite: the coated fiber occupies   . Three estimates: Mori–Tanaka (the matrix is the reference), the self-consistent scheme (every phase a cylinder, the matrix a one-layer one, so that the reference stays transversely isotropic) and the differential scheme. Each is compared with the bare fiber at the same carbon content.

julia
function composite(ϕ, cyl; scheme)
    f = ϕ * (outer_radius(cyl) / a)^2
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, LayeredCylinder((1.0,), (C_matrix,)), Dict(:C => C_matrix); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :fiber, cyl, Dict(:C => C_fiber); fraction = f)
    return ti_constants(best_fit_ti(homogenize(rve, scheme, :C), ez); form = :engineering, role = :stiffness)
end

contents = vcat(0.0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, range(0.04, 0.45; length = 9))
bare = LayeredCylinder((a,), (C_fiber,))
p_ET = plot(; xlabel = "carbon fraction ϕ", ylabel = "E_T (GPa)", legend = :topleft,
    title = "Transverse modulus, a = 3.5 µm, e = 0.5 µm, kₙ = kₜ = 20 GPa/µm", titlefontsize = 10)
for (scheme, name, ls) in ((MoriTanaka(), "Mori-Tanaka", :solid), (SelfConsistent(), "self-consistent", :dash), (DifferentialScheme(), "differential", :dot))
    plot!(p_ET, contents, [composite(ϕ, fiber; scheme)[1] for ϕ in contents]; label = name * ", coated", color = 1, ls)
    plot!(p_ET, contents, [composite(ϕ, bare; scheme)[1] for ϕ in contents]; label = name * ", bare", color = 2, ls)
end
p_ET

The first fibers raise steeply: stiff along the axis, they forbid the axial Poisson contraction of a transverse tension, and the matrix tends to its plane-strain modulus    GPa. Beyond, the soft sizing and its spring lower the transverse modulus; the axial one, carried by the fibers, is the same either way:

julia
for cyl in (bare, fiber)
    E₁, E₃, ν₁₂, ν₃₁, G₃₁ = composite(0.45, cyl; scheme = MoriTanaka())
    @printf("%-6s  E_T = %5.2f GPa   E_L = %6.1f GPa   G_LT = %5.2f GPa\n", cyl === bare ? "bare" : "coated", E₁, E₃, G₃₁)
end

4. The fiber as the limit of a needle ​

A homogeneous prolate ellipsoid of aspect ratio tends to the one-layer cylinder, and so does a confocal layered spheroid of growing focal distance to the layered cylinder with perfect interfaces. An imperfect interface carries a length: the spheroid then tends to the average of the cylinders of its sections, not to the cylinder of its largest radius.

julia
rel(A, B) = norm(canonical_components(A) - canonical_components(B)) / norm(canonical_components(B))
A_cyl = gradient_gradient_concentration(bare, C_fiber, C_matrix)
ωs = 10 .^ range(1, 4; length = 13)
err_ellipsoid = [rel(gradient_gradient_concentration(Ellipsoid(a, a, ω * a), C_fiber, C_matrix), A_cyl) for ω in ωs]
layersK = (TensISO{3}(10.0), TensISO{3}(1.0))
K₀ = TensISO{3}(0.5)
cyl_K = LayeredCylinder((0.6, 1.0), layersK)
A_K = gradient_gradient_concentration(cyl_K, K₀, K₀)
cs = 10 .^ range(0.5, 2.5; length = 9)
err_spheroid = map(cs) do c
    sp = LayeredSpheroid(map(b -> sqrt(b^2 + c^2), (0.6, 1.0)), (0.6, 1.0), layersK; Nseries = 10)
    rel(gradient_gradient_concentration(sp, K₀, K₀), A_K)
end
plot(ωs, err_ellipsoid; xscale = :log10, yscale = :log10, marker = :circle, label = "ellipsoid → cylinder (elasticity), ω = a₃/a",
