Drained and undrained poroelasticity ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/material_laws/03_biot.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/material_laws/03_biot.jl, runs it end to end.

A saturated porous sample is compressed in an oedometer twice: once with its fluid free to leave (drained), once with the fluid trapped (undrained). The same law answers both; only the loading path changes. The law couples two primal fields, the strain and the pore pressure , with their fluxes, the stress and the change of fluid content (see BiotPoroelasticity):

Two tests, one law ​

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization

E, ν, b, M = 10e3, 0.25, 0.8, 5e3        # MPa; Biot coefficient and modulus
law = BiotPoroelasticity(; E, ν, b, M)

oedometer = (ε11 = t -> -1e-3 * t, ε22 = 0, ε33 = 0)          # lateral strains held at zero
drained   = point_response(law, LoadingPath(0:0.25:1; oedometer..., p = 0))   # pore pressure held
undrained = point_response(law, LoadingPath(0:0.25:1; oedometer...))        # φ unnamed: zero
[drained[:σ11][end] undrained[:σ11][end]; drained[:p][end] undrained[:p][end]]
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:
 -12.0  -15.2
   0.0    4.0

In the drained test the pore pressure is prescribed (zero) and the fluid content is free; in the undrained test the fluid content is not named, so it is held at zero, and the pore pressure is what the law makes it.

The same tests on the fractured rock of ARMA 2011 ​

The fractured-reservoir material of Barthélémy and Daniel (2011), built in Building a fractured-rock material, is a material of the same contract with the same two fields. Its stiffness and its Biot parameters come from a homogenization of the rock and its fractures, and its fractures close under compression. The driver runs it along the same paths:

julia
using TensND
C0 = TensISO{3}(3 * 30.0e3, 2 * 18.0e3)                 # intact rock, MPa
rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :M, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C0, :K => TensISO{3}(1e-18)); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :F, PennyCrack(1.0), Dict(:C => C0, :K => TensISO{3}(1e-18)); density = 0.1)
rock = FracturedPoroelasticRock(rve, MoriTanaka(); ω₀ = (1e-3,), k_matrix = 1e-18)

oedometer_z = (ε33 = t -> -1e-5 * t, ε11 = 0, ε22 = 0)
rock_drained   = point_response(rock, LoadingPath(0:0.25:1; oedometer_z..., p = 0))
rock_undrained = point_response(rock, LoadingPath(0:0.25:1; oedometer_z...))
rock_drained[:σ33][end], rock_undrained[:σ33][end], rock_undrained[:p][end]
(-0.3375, -0.54, 0.54)

The undrained sample is again stiffer, and the trapped fluid takes a part of the load as pore pressure. Here the Biot coefficient and modulus are not parameters but results of the homogenization, recomputed as the fractures open or close.
