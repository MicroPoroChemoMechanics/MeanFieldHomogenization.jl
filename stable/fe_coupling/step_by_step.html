Step by step ​

This page builds the quickstart again, one piece at a time, saying for each what it does and what goes wrong without it. Two libraries share the work, and the boundary between them never moves:

Ferrite doesMeanFieldHomogenization does
the mesh, the interpolation, the quadraturethe law at each quadrature point, and its consistent tangent
the numbering of the degrees of freedomthe element: residual and stiffness from the law (material_element!)
the boundary conditions, the assemblythe states of the points (QuadratureStates), the global Newton iteration (quasistatic!)
the VTK file formatwhat goes into it (write_material_vtk)

1. The law ​

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization, Ferrite
a, b, σ₀ = 1.0, 2.0, 250.0
law = J2Plasticity(; E = 200e3, ν = 0.3, σ₀)
J2Plasticity (residual route; ε → σ; internal variables εᵖ, p)

Any material of the Gauss-point contract fits here: a law of MaterialPoints, written by you or shipped, or a homogenized material such as HomogenizedElastic. The rest of the script does not change.

2. The mesh, the interpolation, the quadrature ​

julia
grid = generate_grid(QuadraticQuadrilateral, (8, 6), Vec(0.0, 0.0), Vec(1.0, 1.0))
transform_coordinates!(grid, x -> (r = a + (b - a) * x[1]; θ = π / 2 * x[2]; Vec(r * cos(θ), r * sin(θ))))
ip, ip_geo = Lagrange{RefQuadrilateral, 2}()^2, Lagrange{RefQuadrilateral, 2}()
cv = CellValues(QuadratureRule{RefQuadrilateral}(3), ip, ip_geo)

A square of quadratic quadrilaterals is bent into a quarter of the tube; the facet sets keep their names, "left" becoming the bore and "right" the outer surface.

Quadratic elements for a plastic material

Plastic flow conserves the volume. Linear elements cannot represent a deformation that conserves the volume element by element and lock: they come out far too stiff, and a limit load computed with them is too high. Quadratic elements (or a mixed formulation) avoid it. The geometric interpolation ip_geo must then be quadratic too, or CellValues refuses the curved cells.

3. Degrees of freedom and boundary conditions ​

julia
dh = close!(add!(DofHandler(grid), :u, ip))
ch = ConstraintHandler(dh)
add!(ch, Dirichlet(:u, getfacetset(grid, "bottom"), (x, t) -> 0.0, [2]))
add!(ch, Dirichlet(:u, getfacetset(grid, "top"), (x, t) -> 0.0, [1]))
close!(ch)
ConstraintHandler:
  BCs:
    Field: u, Components: [2]
    Field: u, Components: [1]

On the plane   ("bottom") the displacement normal to it, , vanishes; on   ("top") does. Without them the quarter would be free to slide and turn: the stiffness matrix is singular, and the first Newton step fails.

4. The loads ​

julia
fv = FacetValues(FacetQuadratureRule{RefQuadrilateral}(3), ip, ip_geo)
f_unit = zeros(ndofs(dh))
for facet in FacetIterator(dh, getfacetset(grid, "left"))
    reinit!(fv, facet)
    fe = zeros(ndofs_per_cell(dh))
    for q in 1:getnquadpoints(fv), i in eachindex(fe)
        fe[i] -= (shape_value(fv, q, i) ⋅ getnormal(fv, q)) * getdetJdV(fv, q)
    end
    assemble!(f_unit, celldofs(facet), fe)
end

A pressure pushes on the bore against its outward normal : the traction is  , and its work on each shape function gives the nodal forces of a unit pressure, f_unit. They are computed once; the pressure of each step only scales them.

5. The states of the quadrature points ​

julia
states = QuadratureStates(law, getncells(grid), getnquadpoints(cv))

Each quadrature point remembers its history (here the plastic strain and the cumulated plastic strain), twice: states.old, at the last converged step, which the law reads, and states.new, which the current iteration writes. A converged step is committed (commit!); a rejected one is rolled back (rollback!) and nothing else is needed, since a law never modifies a state it reads. Reading states.new as the start of the next step instead would integrate every Newton iteration as if it were a step: the plastic strain would pile up with each iteration.

6. The system and its Newton iteration ​

julia
p_lim = 2σ₀ / sqrt(3) * log(b / a)
pressure(t) = 0.97 * p_lim * t
system!, prescribe! = material_system(dh, ch, cv, law, PlaneStrain(), states;
    external_forces! = (r, t) -> (r .-= pressure(t) .* f_unit))
u = zeros(ndofs(dh))
history = quasistatic!(system!, prescribe!, u, states, range(0, 1; length = 26))

  • material_system returns the two functions the driver needs: system!(u, t, Δt) assembles, element by element with material_element!, the internal forces minus the external ones and the consistent tangent stiffness; prescribe!(u, t) sets the prescribed displacements.

  • PlaneStrain() says how the two-dimensional strain of an element is a strain of the material; ThreeD and Axisymmetric are the other choices.

  • quasistatic! runs Newton's method on the equilibrium at each time, commits the states of a converged step, and cuts a step that does not converge into halves.

history holds, for each step, the norms of the successive residuals. Here is the last step, at 97 % of the limit load:

julia
norms = history[end][2]
norms ./ norms[1]
6-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.0
 2.460331702688032
 3.1528683252300564
 0.09064202944451677
 4.953301473694487e-5
 1.0019772215124242e-11

The first iterations may grow: so close to the limit load, the extent of the plastic zone is still being found. Once it is, the fall is quadratic, the number of correct digits about doubling at each iteration, which only a tangent consistent with the integration of the law gives. A tangent that is not consistent (an elastic stiffness used in a plastic step, say) converges linearly, one digit at a time, if it converges at all.

7. The results ​

julia
nodal = evaluate_at_grid_nodes(dh, u, :u)
nodal[1][1], pressure(1.0)                       # bore displacement (m) and pressure (MPa)
(0.003404663281844606, 194.09152497289259)

Degrees of freedom are not nodes

Ferrite numbers the degrees of freedom by cell, not by node: u[2k - 1] is not the first displacement of node k. evaluate_at_grid_nodes returns the values at the nodes, in the order of the grid.

write_material_vtk writes the displacement and the cell averages of the stress, the strain and the internal variables, by name, for ParaView:

julia
dir = mktempdir()
write_material_vtk(joinpath(dir, "tube"), dh, u, law, states)
readdir(dir)
1-element Vector{String}:
 "tube.vtu"

GlobalNewtonError at the first stepa rigid-body motion left freeadd the missing boundary conditions
GlobalNewtonError near the limit loadno equilibrium exists beyond itload in displacement, or stop below the limit
slow, linear convergencea tangent not consistent with the lawcheck the law with check_material_interface
a limit load too highvolumetric locking of linear elementsquadratic elements
LocalIntegrationErrora step too large for a lawmore times, or let max_cuts cut the steps
results that do not change with the loadstates.old never committedlet quasistatic! drive, or call commit!

A MaterialCache, which a homogenized material uses to avoid solving the same microstructure twice, is not thread-safe: one per thread, or none.
