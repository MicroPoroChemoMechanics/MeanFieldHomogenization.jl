Plane problems ​

Before this page

Cells and schemes, for the cell and the schemes used here, Cylindrical inclusions, whose sections are the inclusions of the plane, and the theory page Problems of the plane. The tensors of the plane are those of TensND: Two dimensions, and any dimension.

A medium invariant along an axis is studied in the plane normal to it: a fiber composite across its fibers, a rock with parallel channels, a perforated sheet. This page shows how such a two-dimensional medium is homogenized, in plane strain or in plane stress. The cell holds inclusions of the plane and tensors of the plane, isotropic, square, orthotropic or of no symmetry, and homogenize is called as in three dimensions: the schemes are the same, on smaller tensors.

What a cell of space and a cell of the plane accept ​

The rows are the inclusions and the schemes; the columns, the cell of space and the cell of the plane, whose matrix is isotropic in the plane or not.

Why holes of several terms, rings and aging viscoelasticity ask for a matrix isotropic in the plane is said in What is restricted, and why.

Two states, one problem of the plane ​

The axis is and the plane is that of , with Greek indices; in a cell of the plane these two vectors are the first and the second of its basis. In this section the materials are isotropic in the plane: isotropic, or transversely isotropic about the axis. Two states are met in practice.

  • Plane strain,    : a body that is long along the axis. The law of the plane is

    with the plane-strain bulk modulus and the shear modulus of the plane.

  • Plane stress,    : a thin plate loaded in its plane, for the mean fields through its thickness ((Muskhelishvili, 1977), section 26). The axial strain follows from  , and the same law holds with replaced by

For an isotropic material of Young modulus and Poisson ratio ,    in both states,    and   . The two states are thus one problem of the plane, with two moduli, and the stiffness of the plane is the isotropic tensor     of dimension 2, a TensISO{4,2}. TensND builds it from the three-dimensional stiffness:

julia
C = iso_stiffness_young_poisson(70.0, 0.25)                  # E and ν
bulk_shear(plane_strain_stiffness(C)), bulk_shear(plane_stress_stiffness(C))
((56.0, 28.0), (46.66666666666667, 28.0))

A transversely isotropic material is isotropic in the plane normal to its axis:

julia
Cₜ = ti_from_constants(30.0, 60.0, 0.25, 0.2, 9.0, (1.0, 0.0, 0.0); form = :engineering, role = :stiffness)
bulk_shear(plane_stress_stiffness(Cₜ))                       # E₁/(2(1 - ν₁₂)) and E₁/(2(1 + ν₁₂))
(20.0, 12.0)

The same stiffness is built from its two moduli of the plane by iso_stiffness(Val(2), k, μ), never as TensISO{2}(3k, 2μ): the first coefficient of a stiffness of the plane is .

julia
iso_stiffness(Val(2), bulk_shear(plane_strain_stiffness(C))...) ≈ plane_strain_stiffness(C)
true

With symbols the two moduli come out in closed form:

julia
import Symbolics
Symbolics.@variables E ν
Symbolics.simplify.(bulk_shear(plane_stress_stiffness(iso_stiffness_young_poisson(E, ν))))
(E / (2 - 2ν), E / (2(1 + ν)))

The moduli of a tensor of the plane are read back with the same functions. young_poisson returns the constants of the three-dimensional material that has this stiffness in plane strain; plane_strain_to_plane_stress converts them into those of the plate that has it in plane stress, which are also the moduli of the tensor read as a material of the plane, convention = :intrinsic:

julia
c = plane_stress_stiffness(C)
young_poisson(c), plane_strain_to_plane_stress(young_poisson(c)...), young_poisson(c; convention = :intrinsic)
((67.2, 0.20000000000000004), (70.0, 0.25000000000000006), (70.0, 0.25000000000000006))

Which Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio

A TensISO{4,2} is read by default as the plane-strain stiffness of a material of space (convention = :plane_strain): its and are those of that material. With convention = :intrinsic they are the moduli and of a uniaxial stress in the plane, those of the plate for a plane-stress stiffness. The bulk modulus of the plane (bulk_modulus, the first of bulk_shear and of its alias k_mu) and the shear modulus do not depend on the convention; neither do the named coefficients property(:phase, :C, :k) and property(:phase, :C, :μ) of a phase, which are these and (Problems of the plane).

The cell of a plane medium ​

The phases of the cell are inclusions of the plane, and their properties tensors of the plane:

sectionphase
diskEllipsoid(r; dim = 2)
ellipse of semi-axes a and b, turned by angleEllipsoid(a, b; angle)
concentric rings, a coated diskLayeredCylinder(radii, moduli)
hole given by a conformal mapConformalHole
crack, a segment of half-length b turned by angleRibbonCrack(b; angle)

A sheet with circular holes, at the porosity , in plane stress:

julia
disk = Ellipsoid(1.0; dim = 2)
function sheet(c, f; hole = disk, kw...)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, disk, Dict(:C => c); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :holes, hole, Dict(:C => 0 * c); fraction = f, kw...)
    return rve
end
homogenize(sheet(c, 0.1), MoriTanaka(), :C)

The estimate is a tensor of the plane, in the state of the properties given: here the plane-stress stiffness of the perforated sheet. For circular holes Young's modulus of the sheet does not depend on Poisson's ratio, and the schemes have the closed forms    (Mori and Tanaka),   (self-consistent) and   (differential):

end

Sections that are not disks ​

A hole given by a conformal map is declared as in three dimensions (Conformal holes); in a cell of the plane it is its section. The estimate is then a general tensor of the plane; a section with an axis of symmetry of order 3, or of order 5 and more, leaves it isotropic, and its moduli are read on its isotropic part. Triangular holes:

julia
triangle = ConformalHole(polygon_map(3, 30)...)

Coated disks ​

A coated fiber seen across its axis is a disk in a ring. It is declared as in three dimensions, by its radii and by the properties of its layers, here tensors of the plane (Layered inclusions); the interfaces between the layers are given in the same way. A stiff fiber in a soft coating:

julia
fiber = plane_stress_stiffness(iso_stiffness_young_poisson(200.0, 0.3))
coating = plane_stress_stiffness(iso_stiffness_young_poisson(20.0, 0.35))
coated = LayeredCylinder((0.8, 1.0), (fiber, coating))        # radii, then the layers from the center
rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :matrix, disk, Dict(:C => c); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :fibers, coated, Dict(:C => fiber); fraction = 0.3)
bulk_shear(homogenize(rve, MoriTanaka(), :C)), bulk_shear(c)
((45.35206332167336, 26.19218349736338), (46.66666666666667, 28.0))

Cracks ​

A crack of the plane is a segment of half-length , the trace of a ribbon crack unbounded along the normal to the plane: RibbonCrack(b; angle), with angle the angle of the segment with the first axis. As in three dimensions (Cracks) its amount is a density, here    for cracks per unit area. Its opening under a unit stress is the tensor , proportional to the identity in an isotropic plane:

that is   in plane strain and in plane stress.

julia
crack = RibbonCrack(1.0; angle = 0.0)

([0.04487989505128275 0.0; 0.0 0.04487989505128275], 0.04487989505128276)

The intensity factors at its tips, in opening and in sliding, are     under the remote stress :

An anisotropic matrix of the plane ​

An ellipse and a segment accept a matrix of any anisotropy of the plane: the Hill tensor of the ellipse is computed by residues (Ellipses of the plane), and the opening of the segment as that of the ribbon crack in the medium invariant along the axis. An elliptic hole given by its map, ConformalHole((m,)), accepts it too, and has the concentration tensor of the ellipse of the same semi-axes (Conformal holes); its potentials give besides the stress along its edge, contour_stress, and in the matrix around it, matrix_stress. Stiff ellipses in the orthotropic matrix c₀:

julia
c₁ = iso_stiffness(Val(2), 50.0, 20.0)                # k and μ of the plane
function composite(c₀; inclusion = Ellipsoid(1.0, 0.4), f = 0.2)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, disk, Dict(:C => c₀); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :inclusions, inclusion, Dict(:C => c₁); fraction = f)
    return rve
end

A coefficient of the class of a tensor is named in property: :C1111, :C2222, :C1122, :C1212 (and :C2211 without the major symmetry) for an orthotropic stiffness, :a1, :a2 for an orthotropic conductivity, :α, :β, :γ for a square stiffness, its coefficients on the three projectors of the square class, and :k, :μ for an isotropic one. A general tensor is read in a class of the plane that contains it, with symmetry = (:ORTHO, Basis(θ)) or (:CUBIC, Basis(θ)); (:TI, …) is refused, the plane having no such class. The same medium in three dimensions ​

A medium invariant along an axis is also a three-dimensional cell of aligned cylinders (Cylinder, LayeredCylinder, ConformalHole, with three-dimensional properties), which is in plane strain in the plane of the sections. Its estimate carries, besides the moduli of the plane, the axial modulus and the shear modulus along the axis; its block of the plane is the estimate of the plane cell.

julia
function prisms(f)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Cylinder(1.0, 1.0), Dict(:C => C); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :holes, Cylinder(1.0, 1.0), Dict(:C => 0 * C); fraction = f)
    return rve
end
C₃ = canonical_components(homogenize(prisms(0.1), SelfConsistent(), :C))    # axis 1, plane (2, 3)
C₂ = canonical_components(homogenize(sheet(plane_strain_stiffness(C), 0.1), SelfConsistent(), :C))

The plane cell is the route to take when the moduli of the plane are what is wanted: it carries tensors of dimension 2 only, and it is the one that gives plane stress.

Every scheme, and what follows from the estimate ​

All the schemes of Homogenization schemes take a cell of the plane. Those that need the shape of the spatial distribution of the inclusions are given a shape of the plane:

    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, disk, Dict(:C => c); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :fibers, disk, Dict(:C => stiff); fraction = 0.2)

end

Disks distributed as disks give the estimate of Mori and Tanaka, as spheres distributed as spheres do in space. What is computed from an estimate applies as well: the concentration tensors of the phases (concentration_tensors), the compliances of the families of cracks (crack_family_compliances), and the poroelastic coefficients (Poromechanics). A cell is of one dimension ​

The phases and the properties of a cell belong to the same space. A sphere in a cell of the plane, or a tensor of the plane given to a spheroid, is refused with a message that names the two dimensions:

julia
try
    gradient_gradient_concentration(Ellipsoid(1.0), 0 * c, c)        # a sphere, a tensor of the plane
catch err
    print(first(split(sprint(showerror, err), ". ")))
end
ArgumentError: an inclusion of type Ellipsoid, of dimension 3, cannot be a phase of a cell whose properties are tensors of dimension 2

What is restricted, and why ​

Holes given by a map of several terms and concentric rings take a matrix isotropic in the plane, in elasticity and in conduction, and the rings layers isotropic in the plane. The hole is solved with the complex potentials of Kolosov and Muskhelishvili, the rings with radial problems that separate harmonic by harmonic: both rest on the isotropy of the plane. An anisotropic plane needs the potentials of Lekhnitskii, functions of one complex variable per characteristic root, and couples the harmonics of the rings. The variable of the hole and the two of Lekhnitskii are brought to the same circle by one map for the ellipse only: the elliptic hole is solved in any matrix of the plane (The elliptic hole in an anisotropic matrix), the hole of several terms and the rings are not. For these two, a general tensor that is isotropic to a relative , as the estimate of a scheme can be, is accepted; a symbolic one must be given as a TensISO. Otherwise the inclusion refuses the matrix:

julia
try
    gradient_gradient_concentration(triangle, 0 * c₀, c₀)
catch err
    print(first(split(sprint(showerror, err), "; ")))
end
ArgumentError: an inclusion of the plane takes a property that is isotropic in the plane

An ellipse, the elliptic hole included in elasticity and in conduction, and a segment accept any anisotropy of the plane. The other restrictions are these.

  • Aging viscoelasticity takes a matrix isotropic in the plane: its Hill kernel is known against an isotropic reference only. The schemes it applies are listed in Cells of the plane.

  • The plane has no transversely isotropic class: TISymmetrize and symmetry = (:TI, …) are refused in a cell of the plane.

  • Laminates and conducting cracks (ConductiveCrack) are not written in the plane.

  • The average over the rotations alone of a section without a mirror line is not available: IsoSymmetrize also averages over the reflections.

  • The inclusions that are three-dimensional by nature, spheres and spheroids with layers, tori, the shapes solved by a grid, a mesh or a network, have no counterpart in the plane.
