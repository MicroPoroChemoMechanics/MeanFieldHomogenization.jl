Heterogeneities of any shape ​

Before this page

Custom inclusions, whose level B this inclusion implements, and Concentration and contribution tensors for the two tensors it returns.

A shape with no closed form is treated in three ways, on this page and on the two that follow: its integral equation is solved on a grid of Gaussians, its Eshelby problem is solved on a mesh (Finite-element inclusions), or a network trained on such solutions is evaluated (Neural-surrogate inclusions). The three enter the schemes through the custom-inclusion interface; the table at the end of this page says which to choose.

GaussianInclusion is a heterogeneity whose shape is any region of space: a torus, a concave particle, a shape given by a function or by voxels. Its concentration tensors are computed by the Gaussian approximation of its integral equation, in elasticity and in conduction. It needs neither a mesh nor an extension.

Describing a shape ​

A shape is an AbstractRegion of space, built as the contour of a planar crack is:

ConstructorRegion
Region(Ellipsoid(a, b, c))an ellipsoid, with its orientation
Region(Supersphere(a, p)), Region(Superspheroid(a, c, p))a supersphere, a superspheroid
Torus(R, a)a torus
Region(inside, lo, hi)any function inside(x), in the box of corners lo, hi
PixelRegion(mask, h)voxels: a Boolean array and the size of a voxel
∪, ∩, setdiffset operations on regions

GaussianInclusion also takes an Ellipsoid, a Supersphere or a Superspheroid directly.

julia
surface = param_surface((u, v) -> ((1 + 0.35cos(v)) * cos(u), (1 + 0.35cos(v)) * sin(u), 0.35sin(v)),
    range(0, 2π; length = 61), range(0, 2π; length = 25))
plotly_scene([surface_trace(surface...; color = "#4063D8")]; uid = "man-gauss-torus", height = 380,
    title = "Torus(1.0, 0.35)")
julia
torus = GaussianInclusion(Torus(1.0, 0.35); n = 16)
GaussianInclusion (Region, 780 nodes, h=0.16875)

n is the number of nodes along the longest side of the box of the region. The slice of the torus through its axis, as the grid sees it (the fraction of each cell that the region covers):

julia
region = Torus(1.0, 0.35)
grid = GaussianGrid(region, 48)
θ = coverage(region, grid; samples = 3)
heatmap(θ[:, 24, :]'; aspect_ratio = :equal, color = :grays, colorbar = false, axis = false)

Concentration tensors ​

The two tensors of the interface, for a heterogeneity of property P₁ in a matrix of property P₀:

julia
K₀, K₁ = TensISO{3}(1.0), TensISO{3}(50.0)
A = gradient_gradient_concentration(torus, K₁, K₀)       # mean gradient / remote gradient
round.(get_array(A); sigdigits = 3)
3×3 Matrix{Float64}:
  0.171     -2.5e-16   -5.93e-14
 -5.34e-16   0.171     -5.59e-14
 -1.23e-12   2.86e-12   0.0416

The mean gradient is four times larger in the plane of the torus than along its axis. One solve gives both tensors and is kept by the inclusion for the pair of properties:

julia
flux_gradient_concentration(torus, K₁, K₀)
fe_assembly_count(torus)
1

In a scheme ​

julia
rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:K => K₀); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :tori, torus, Dict(:K => K₁); fraction = 0.1, symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize())
k_tori = first(get_data(homogenize(rve, MoriTanaka(), :K)))

rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:K => K₀); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :spheres, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:K => K₁); fraction = 0.1)
k_spheres = first(get_data(homogenize(rve, MoriTanaka(), :K)))
k_tori, k_spheres
(1.6866786812256094, 1.3121019108280256)

At the same volume fraction, randomly oriented tori raise the conductivity more than spheres do.

Accuracy and cost ​

The error of the default grid is a few percent against Eshelby's solution, for contrasts between and (theory, §4). On a tilted ellipsoid fifty times more conducting than the matrix:

julia
exact = Ellipsoid(1.0, 0.7, 0.5; euler_angles = (0.4, 0.9, 0.0))
A_exact = gradient_gradient_concentration(exact, K₁, K₀)
for n in (12, 16, 24)
    A_n = gradient_gradient_concentration(GaussianInclusion(exact; n), K₁, K₀)
    println("n = ", n, "   relative error: ", round(norm(A_n - A_exact) / norm(A_exact); sigdigits = 2))
end
n = 12   relative error: 0.11
n = 16   relative error: 0.047
n = 24   relative error: 0.018

A solve takes seconds in conduction and tens of seconds in elasticity at 24 to 32 nodes; its memory grows as and the inclusion is refused, before anything is allocated, when it would exceed memory_limit. Three limits:

  • the reference medium is isotropic (symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize() otherwise);

  • a contrast beyond on the stiff side is slow, and already gives the rigid limit within the error of the method;

  • a derivative with respect to the shape is refused; derivatives with respect to the properties are available through ForwardDiff.

Choosing among the three ​

NeedPage
any shape, no mesh, a few percentGaussianInclusion, this page
an axisymmetric pore, to the accuracy of a meshFinite-element inclusions
many evaluations, differentiable in the shapeNeural-surrogate inclusions
a flat crack of any contourPlanar cracks of any contour

Choosing an inclusion sets these beside the closed forms and the layered patterns, with what each route allows.
