The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/78_insulating_torus.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/78_insulating_torus.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Toroidal inclusions, which derives the tensor used here, and Toroidal inclusions, which gives the syntax.

A toroidal pore conducts nothing and has no Hill tensor. Its concentration tensor is a series of toroidal functions, obtained by Radi and Sevostianov and computed in the package by InsulatingTorus. This page evaluates it against the ratio of the two radii, compares it with the values plotted in that article and with spheroids of the same volume, and gives the conductivity of a matrix that contains such pores in random orientations.

The torus beside spheroids of the same volume ​

The article compares the torus with two spheroids of the same axis and the same volume. The oblate one has the outer radius   of the torus for equatorial radius, hence the aspect ratio    . The prolate one has the radius of the tube for equatorial radius, hence  . Their tensors are exact, through the Hill tensor of the Ellipsoid:

julia
oblate(λ) = Ellipsoid(1.0, 1.0, 3π * λ^2 / (2 * (1 + λ)^3))
prolate(λ) = Ellipsoid(1.0, 1.0, 3π / (2λ))
sphere = first(components(Ellipsoid(1.0)))            ## 3/2

Ao, Ap = components.(oblate.(λs)), components.(prolate.(λs))

Neither spheroid has the two components of the torus. The oblate one comes close to it when the hole closes and tends to a disk-shaped crack, whose component along the axis is unbounded, when the ring is thin. The prolate one tends to a needle along the axis, with the components 2 across it and 1 along it, where the thin ring has and 2.

The mean of the three principal components is another matter. It is the only quantity that enters the conductivity of a matrix with randomly oriented pores, and that of the needle,   , is that of the thin ring,   : a ring is a wire, and a wire in random orientations does not remember that it was bent.

julia
mean_component(a) = (2 * a[1] + a[2]) / 3
plot(
    λs, [mean_component.(A) mean_component.(Ao) mean_component.(Ap)];
    label = ["torus" "oblate spheroid" "prolate spheroid"], lw = 2, ls = [:solid :dash :dot],
    xlabel = "λ = r / R", ylabel = "(2Aₜ + Aₙ) / 3", ylims = (1.4, 2.4), legend = :topright,
    title = "Mean component, the one seen by randomly oriented pores", titlefontsize = 10, size = (640, 360)
)
hline!([sphere]; color = :gray, lw = 1, label = "sphere")

The largest relative deviation of the prolate spheroid from the torus, on the mean component and over the range of the plot:

julia
maximum(abs.(mean_component.(Ap) ./ mean_component.(A) .- 1))
0.017506112651535233

For randomly oriented toroidal pores, the prolate spheroid of the same volume and of the same tube radius is thus a close substitute, as the article concludes; for aligned ones it is not, and the series is needed.

Conductivity of a matrix with randomly oriented toroidal pores ​

The pores occupy the volume fraction . symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize() averages the tensor of the phase over the orientations (Choosing an inclusion); the effective conductivity is then isotropic.

julia
function porous(λ, c)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:K => K₀); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :pores, torus(λ), Dict(:K => void); fraction = c, symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize())
    return rve
end
conductivity(λ, c, scheme) = canonical_components(homogenize(porous(λ, c), scheme, :K))[1, 1]

The self-consistent estimate has no such form: it places the pores and the matrix, the latter as spherical grains, in the effective medium itself, and vanishes at a finite fraction of pores.

julia

    "Mori-Tanaka" => MoriTanaka(),

A toroidal pore lowers the conductivity a little more than a spherical one of the same volume, by about the same amount whatever its thickness. What sets it apart is its anisotropy when the pores are aligned: with all the axes along the conductivity is transversely isotropic,
