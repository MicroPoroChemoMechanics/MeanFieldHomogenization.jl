The affine formulation for elasto-viscoplastic composites ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/material_laws/19_affine_evp.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/material_laws/19_affine_evp.jl, runs it end to end.

The affine formulation linearizes phases whose stress is a function of the strain alone. Pierard and Doghri (2006) extend it to phases with a history: elastic, viscoplastic, and hardening with their accumulated strain. Linearized in time, such a phase is a linear viscoelastic solid with an eigenstrain, which the Laplace–Carson transform turns into a thermoelastic one, so that a linear scheme can concentrate the strain. This page states the formulation for a matrix with inclusions and the Mori–Tanaka scheme, as in the paper. The example below runs it.

The step ​

The phases are integrated over the step at the average strains and  , which sets their linearizations and so the concentration; the strain of the inclusions it returns must be the one they were integrated at. The package iterates this fixed point by successive substitutions, as the paper does, accelerated as Anderson (1965) proposes. The macroscopic stress is the average of the stresses of the phases, the first of the two choices of the paper (its section 3.4). The consistent tangent follows by the implicit-function theorem, from the tangents of the phases and the derivative of the concentration.

Scope ​

phasesa matrix and inclusions of laws in rate form: PerzynaViscoplasticity, NortonViscoplasticity
schemeMori–Tanaka, the Eshelby tensor in the isotropic part of the matrix modulus (isotropic = false takes the whole modulus)
checkstwo identical phases give the phase; elastic phases the elastic Mori–Tanaka estimate; linear Maxwell phases the linear viscoelastic one, computed in closed form, which the steps approach at the first order of their backward Euler scheme
not coveredthe second moments of the strain as reference of the linearization, the macroscopic stress from the viscoelastic relation of the comparison composite (the second choice of the paper), more than two phases

Example: a particle-reinforced elasto-viscoplastic composite ​

A matrix holds 20 % of stiffer and stronger spherical particles, both of Perzyna type with linear hardening, pulled at a constant strain rate with free lateral faces.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization, TensND, Plots

f = 0.2
matrix = PerzynaViscoplasticity(; E = 70e3, ν = 0.3, σY = 100.0, η = 1e5, m = 2.0, hardening = p -> 2e3 * p)
particles = PerzynaViscoplasticity(; E = 400e3, ν = 0.25, σY = 500.0, η = 1e6, m = 2.0, hardening = p -> 1e4 * p)
rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :M, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => TensISO{3}(1.0, 1.0)); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :P, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => TensISO{3}(1.0, 1.0)); fraction = f)
laws = (M = matrix, P = particles)
θ = exp10.(range(-3, 3; length = 20))                          # relaxation times of the collocation, s
codetheory
AffineEVPComposite(rve, laws; relaxation_times = θ)the affine formulation of Pierard and Doghri, Mori–Tanaka on the comparison composite
IncrementalTangent(rve, laws)the incremental tangent approach, the consistent tangents projected on the isotropic tensors
UniformStrain, UniformStressthe uniform-field composites

Tension ​

A strain rate of   up to 1 %:

julia
pull = LoadingPath(range(0, 10; length = 41); ε11 = t -> 1e-3 * t)
p = plot(; xlabel = "axial strain (%)", ylabel = "axial stress (MPa)", legend = :bottomright)
for (c, label, ls) in ((UniformStrain(laws, (M = 1 - f, P = f)), "uniform strain", :dash),
                       (IncrementalTangent(rve, laws), "incremental tangent", :solid),
                       (AffineEVPComposite(rve, laws; relaxation_times = θ), "affine", :solid),
                       (UniformStress(laws, (M = 1 - f, P = f)), "uniform stress", :dash))
    r = point_response(c, pull)
    plot!(p, r[:ε11] * 100, r[:σ11]; lw = 2, ls, label)
end
p

The affine estimate lies below the incremental tangent one, which Pierard and Doghri (2006) found too stiff when the tangent of a time step is used for elasto-viscoplastic phases, and between the uniform-field bounds.

Check ​

Two identical phases are the phase itself:

julia
c = AffineEVPComposite(rve, (M = matrix, P = matrix); relaxation_times = θ)
point_response(c, pull)[:σ11] ≈ point_response(matrix, pull)[:σ11]
true
