API — MaterialPoints ​

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints Module
julia
MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints

Constitutive laws at a material point: writing a nonlinear law in a few lines, integrating it over a step with its consistent tangent, driving it along a loading path, and plugging it into any caller of the Gauss-point contract, a finite-element code included.

Four independent pieces

piecewhat it sayshow
the lawwhat the material isResidualLaw (local equations) or PotentialLaw (free energy and dissipation), parameters in p
the local algorithmhow a step is integratedLocalNewton, with the tangent by the implicit-function theorem
the loadingalong which pathLoadingPath, each component driven in strain or in stress
the couplingwhere the law is evaluatedpoint_response at one point, material_response in an element loop

Any law runs with any algorithm, along any path, in any caller. A law is a AbstractMFHMaterial like a homogenized material, so the coupling code of the package serves both.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization

law = J2Plasticity(; E = 200e3, ν = 0.3, σ₀ = 250.0, H = 2e3)
path = LoadingPath(range(0, 1; length = 101); ε11 = t -> 5e-3 * t)   # uniaxial tension
r = point_response(law, path)
r[:σ11], r[:p]                        # stress and cumulated plastic strain

Numbers

Strains and stresses are Mandel vectors (SVector{6}), tangents SMatrix{6, 6}: a step allocates nothing. Every number may be a ForwardDiff.Dual, nested or not, in the strain, the state or the parameters: the response then carries its derivatives, exact (no finite differences). The small-strain kinematics (SmallStrain) is the only one so far.

Writing a law ​

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.AbstractPointLaw Type
julia
AbstractPointLaw <: AbstractMFHMaterial

Supertype of the constitutive laws of MaterialPoints: a law written with ResidualLaw or PotentialLaw, integrated over a step by point_update.

Being an AbstractMFHMaterial, a law answers material_response and plugs into any caller of that contract, a finite-element element loop included, exactly as a homogenized material does.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.ResidualLaw Type
julia
ResidualLaw(; fluxes, residual, layout, gradients = (ε = 6,), flux_names = (σ = 6,),
              p = (;), elastic = nothing, y₀ = zeros, name = :ResidualLaw)
ResidualLaw(; stress, residual, layout, …)

A law given by the equations of its implicit integration over a step.

  • fluxes(g, y, p) returns the fluxes, a NamedTuple by the flux names: (σ = …,) for a mechanical law, (σ = …, φ = …) for a poroelastic one. A mechanical law may give stress(ε, y, p) instead.

  • residual(y, g, prev, Δt, p) returns the residual of the local equations, a NamedTuple by the names of the internal variables (or a flat SVector), whose root is the state at the end of the step, in any units.

  • layout names the internal variables and their sizes, e.g. (εᵖ = 6, p = 1), or (d = 1,) for a scalar damage.

  • gradients, flux_names: the primal fields and their conjugate fluxes, with their sizes, in the same order. The default is the strain and the stress.

  • p holds every parameter the functions read.

  • elastic(g, prev, Δt, p), optional, returns true when the step leaves the internal variables unchanged: the elastic predictor of a rate-independent law, or the threshold of a damage law. Without it every step is solved.

  • y₀, optional, the initial internal variables (zero by default).

The functions receive g, y and the previous state prev (fields prev.y, prev.g) as NamedTuples: g.ε, g.p, y.εᵖ, prev.y.d. A size-6 entry is a symmetric tensor in Mandel components, a size-1 entry a scalar.

point_update solves the residual by Newton's method with a Jacobian by automatic differentiation, and returns the consistent tangent of the fluxes with respect to the gradients by the implicit-function theorem,

every coupling block included, so that no derivative is written by hand.

julia
# A viscous shear flow in series with a spring: σ = 2μ (ε − εᵛ), ε̇ᵛ = dev σ / (2η)
law = ResidualLaw(;
    stress = (ε, y, p) -> 2p.μ * (ε - y.εᵛ),
    residual = (y, g, prev, Δt, p) -> (εᵛ = y.εᵛ - prev.y.εᵛ - Δt * deviator(2p.μ * (g.ε - y.εᵛ)) / (2p.η),),
    layout = (εᵛ = 6,), p = (μ = 1.0, η = 10.0),
)
MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.PotentialLaw Type
julia
PotentialLaw(; energy, dissipation = nothing, layout = (;), gradients = (ε = 6,),
               flux_names = (σ = 6,), signs = nothing, p = (;), y₀ = zeros,
               name = :PotentialLaw)

A law given by two potentials, in the framework of generalized standard materials: the free energy of the gradients and the internal variables, and the dissipation potential .

  • energy(g, α, p) returns ;

  • dissipation(α̇, α, p) returns , α being the internal variables at the beginning of the step; it is required as soon as the law has internal variables;

  • each flux is the derivative of with respect to its gradient, times the sign signs gives it (+1 by default): a poroelastic potential written in strain and pore pressure gives   and   , hence signs = (σ = 1, φ = -1);

  • layout, gradients, flux_names, p, y₀ and name as for ResidualLaw.

Over a step of length the internal variables minimize the incremental energy

the stationarity condition being solved as the residual of a ResidualLaw: the gradient and the Hessian come from automatic differentiation, so a law is written as its two potentials and nothing else. Newton's method finds a stationary point; with convex potentials it is the minimum. A step of zero length leaves the internal variables unchanged.

julia
# Nonlinear elasticity, no internal variable
law = PotentialLaw(; energy = (g, α, p) -> p.k / 2 * trace_mandel(g.ε)^2 + p.μ * sum(abs2, deviator(g.ε))^2,
                   p = (k = 10.0, μ = 3.0))
MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.PointState Type
julia
PointState(y, g) <: AbstractMaterialState

The state a material point carries from one step to the next: its internal variables y and its gradients g (the strain, and any other primal field of the law) at the end of the step, both flat SVectors laid out as the law's state_layout and gradient_layout say.

The functions of a law read the state of the previous step by name, as prev.y.εᵖ or prev.g.ε. A state is never mutated: point_update returns a new one, so that a step a caller rejects is retried from the old one.

Integrating a step ​

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.AbstractLocalAlgorithm Type
julia
AbstractLocalAlgorithm

Supertype of the algorithms that integrate a law over a step. LocalNewton is the default; a new algorithm subtypes this and adds a method of MaterialPoints._solve_local.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.LocalNewton Type
julia
LocalNewton(; abstol = 1e-12, reltol = 1e-12, maxiters = 50)

Newton's method on the local equations of a law, with the Jacobian by automatic differentiation.

The step is converged when the Newton correction of the internal variables satisfies   abstol reltol . The correction has the units of the internal variables whatever the units of the residual, so the residual of a law may be written in any units: the equations of a ResidualLaw in strain units and the stationarity condition of a PotentialLaw, in units of stress, converge alike. After maxiters iterations without convergence, or at a singular Jacobian, a LocalIntegrationError is thrown, which a driver catches to cut the step.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.LocalIntegrationError Type
julia
LocalIntegrationError(law, iterations, correction, reason)

Thrown by point_update when the local equations of a law could not be solved: the law's name, the iterations done, the last Newton correction (largest entry) and the reason. A driver that catches it can retry the step in smaller pieces, as point_response does; any other error is a bug and is not caught.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.point_update Function
julia
point_update(law, g, prev, Δt; algorithm = LocalNewton(), external = (;)) -> (F, 𝕂, state)

Integrate law over a step of length Δt that brings its gradients to g, from the PointState prev. Returns the fluxes F, the consistent tangent 𝕂 = dF/dg and the new state.

g and F are flat SVectors laid out as gradient_layout and flux_layout say: for a mechanical law, the strain and the stress as Mandel vectors and 𝕂 the 6×6 tangent stiffness; for a coupled law, the fields one after the other and 𝕂 with every coupling block.

This is the function every caller ends up in: material_response wraps it for the TensND contract, point_response drives it along a loading path, an element loop calls it at each quadrature point. Its inputs and outputs are static arrays and it allocates nothing.

external gives, by name, the variables the law reads without returning their flux at the end of the step: a temperature (T = 350.0,), say. The law reads them in p, beside its parameters.

The numbers may be ForwardDiff.Dual, in g, in prev, in Δt, in the law's parameters or in external, nested or not: the fluxes, the tangent and the state then carry the derivatives, exactly.

The law of a composite whose phases follow nonlinear laws, homogenized by a secant method. rve is a cell of the package whose phases carry an isotropic stiffness :C (any value for a phase given a law: it is replaced), laws maps phase names to laws of MaterialPoints without internal variable (nonlinear elastic, e.g. PowerLawElasticity); a phase without a law keeps its linear stiffness. scheme is any linear homogenization scheme of the package, method a SecantMethod.

At the macroscopic strain , every phase with a law takes its secant moduli at its effective strain , the linear composite they form is homogenized into , and

a fixed point in the effective strains. The result is a ResidualLaw whose internal variables are those effective strains, one per phase with a law, named after the phases: the consistent tangent of the composite follows from the implicit-function theorem, and the law runs in point_response and in a finite-element computation like any other.

How the effective strain of a phase is measured in a secant method: ClassicalSecant by its average, ModifiedSecant by its second moment.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.ClassicalSecant Type
julia
ClassicalSecant()

The effective strain of a phase is the equivalent strain of its average strain,   , being the strain concentration tensor of the phase in the linear comparison composite.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.ModifiedSecant Type
julia
ModifiedSecant()

The effective strain of a phase is the square root of the second moment of its equivalent strain,

the derivative of the effective stiffness of the linear comparison composite with respect to the shear modulus of the phase giving the energy the phase stores (Kreher, 1990). It is the method whose estimates coincide with the variational ones of Ponte Castañeda (1991) for power-law phases (see the theory page).

Driving a point ​

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.LoadingPath Type
julia
LoadingPath(times; ε11 = …, σ22 = …, p = …, …)

A loading path for a material point: the times at which the response is computed, and, for each component of each field of the law, whether it is driven by its gradient or by its flux. A value is a number (held constant) or a function of time.

  • A tensor field is driven component by component: ε11, …, ε12 for the strain, σ11, …, σ12 for the stress (tensor components: ε12 is , not the engineering shear ).

  • A scalar field by its name: p for the pore pressure, φ for the change of fluid content of a BiotPoroelasticity law.

  • A component named in neither form has a zero flux: a free face.

  • external gives the variables the law reads without returning their flux, by name, as numbers or functions of time: external = (T = t -> 293 + 0.5t,) for a temperature (see point_update).

julia
tension   = LoadingPath(range(0, 1; length = 101); ε11 = t -> 2e-3 * t)   # uniaxial stress
creep     = LoadingPath(range(0, 100; length = 201); σ11 = 150.0)          # constant stress
drained   = LoadingPath(0:0.1:1; ε11 = t -> 1e-3 * t, p = 0)              # pore pressure held at zero
undrained = LoadingPath(0:0.1:1; ε11 = t -> 1e-3 * t)                     # φ = 0: no fluid exchanged

The first time is the initial instant: the response there is computed from the initial state with a step of zero length. The names are checked against the law's fields when the path is run.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.point_response Function
julia
point_response(material, path::LoadingPath; algorithm = LocalNewton(), state = initial_state(material),
               rtol = 1e-10, maxiters = 25, max_cuts = 8, on_step = nothing) -> PointResponse

Drive a material point along path and return its response at every time of the path.

material is a law of MaterialPoints or any material of the Gauss-point contract (AbstractMFHMaterial). At each time the components the path drives by their flux are matched to a relative residual rtol by Newton's method on the free gradients, with the consistent tangent; when the local integration or this iteration fails, the step is retried in two halves, down to max_cuts halvings.

on_step(i, t, g, F, state), if given, is called after each time of the path, for instance to write the response to a file as it is computed, so that an interrupted run keeps what it had.

julia
law = J2Plasticity(; E = 200e3, ν = 0.3, σ₀ = 250.0, H = 1e3)
r = point_response(law, LoadingPath(range(0, 1; length = 51); ε11 = t -> 5e-3 * t))
r[:σ11], r[:εᵖ11]
MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.PointResponse Type
julia
PointResponse

What point_response returns: the times t, and at each of them the gradients g, the fluxes F (flat vectors, laid out as the law's fields) and the state. Indexed by name:

  • r[:t], the times;

  • a component of a field, by the names LoadingPath uses: r[:ε11], r[:σ12] (tensor components, the √2 of the shears removed), r[:p], r[:φ];

  • an internal variable, by the name the law's state_layout gives it: r[:p] would be shadowed by a field of that name, so a plastic law calls its cumulated strain p only when no field does; a six-entry one also by component, r[:εᵖ11].

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.QuadratureStates Type
julia
QuadratureStates(material, ncells, nqp)

The states of the quadrature points of a mesh, nqp per cell, kept twice:

  • states.old[cell][qp], the state at the last converged step, which the material reads;

  • states.new[cell][qp], the state the current iteration writes.

commit! makes the new states the old ones once a step has converged; rollback! discards them when it is rejected. A material never mutates a state, so nothing else is needed to retry a step.

MeanFieldHomogenization.MaterialPoints.quasistatic! Function
julia
quasistatic!(system!, prescribe!, u, states, times; rtol = 1e-8, maxiters = 25,
             max_cuts = 8, on_step = nothing) -> history

Drive a structure through the load steps times, by Newton's method on its equilibrium with the consistent tangent of its materials, independently of the finite-element library.

  • prescribe!(u, t) sets the prescribed degrees of freedom of u at time t;

  • system!(u, t, Δt) assembles, at the displacement u, the Jacobian K and the residual r (internal minus external forces) of the step ending at t, the rows of the prescribed degrees of freedom set to those of the identity and to zero; it reads the states from states.old and writes them into states.new, and returns (K, r);

  • u is updated in place, states a QuadratureStates.

A step is converged when   rtol times the norm of its first residual. It is then committed; otherwise, or when a material fails to integrate (LocalIntegrationError), the step is rolled back and retried in two halves, down to max_cuts halvings. on_step(i, t, u, states) is called after each time of times. Returns, for each step taken (cut steps included), the time and the norms of its successive residuals, from which the quadratic convergence of a consistent tangent can be read.
