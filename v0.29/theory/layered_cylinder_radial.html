Layered cylinder: layers transversely isotropic about the radius ​

Before this page

Layered cylinder, whose loading modes, interfaces and averages are kept here, and The radial problem, which solves a mode once its matrix is known.

A layer of a LayeredCylinder may be transversely isotropic about the axis of the fiber, as the previous pages assume, or about the radial direction of the section: a wound sheet, a coating deposited on a fiber, the rings of a trunk. Its plane of isotropy then turns around the axis. This page gives the matrix of each loading mode for such a layer; with moduli that depend on it is the matrix of a graded one. The matrices are derived symbolically in the companion tutorial.

5. Chaining and averages ​

6. The parameters of a layer, and second moments ​

A coefficient of a layer is designated as for the sphere (Layered sphere: layers transversely isotropic about the radius, sections 6 and 7), by its name in the frame of the cylindrical chart:

julia
rve = RVE()
add_phase!(rve, :M, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); fraction = :rest)
add_phase!(rve, :I, LayeredCylinder((0.6, 1.0), (C₁, wound)), Dict(:C => C₁); fraction = 0.3)
axial_modulus(C) = canonical_components(C)[3, 3, 3, 3]
[name => derivative(rve, MoriTanaka(), layer_param(:I, 2, name); indexer = axial_modulus) for name in (:Crrrr, :Cθθθθ, :Cθθzz, :Crrθθ, :Crθrθ)]

7. Scope ​

  • Symmetry. A layer is transversely isotropic about the axis of the cylinder or about its radial direction; any other class is refused.

  • Numbers. Real, complex and dual numbers, nested.

  • Not available with radial or graded layers: the pointwise fields and aging viscoelasticity.
