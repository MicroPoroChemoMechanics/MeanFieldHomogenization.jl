Toroidal inclusions ​

Before this page

Toroidal inclusions, where the tensor returned below is derived, and Custom inclusions for the interface through which it enters the schemes.

A toroidal pore is a ring-shaped cavity: the torus swept by a circle of radius whose center describes a circle of radius  . InsulatingTorus is this pore in the conduction problem, in an isotropic matrix. Its field is not uniform and it has no Hill tensor; its concentration tensor is the sum of a series, exact to rounding.

Declaring a torus ​

The two radii, then the axis, which is the third vector of the basis given by the ZYZ euler_angles (or by basis):

julia
torus = InsulatingTorus(1.0, 0.35)
tilted = InsulatingTorus(1.0, 0.35; euler_angles = (0.0, π / 3, 0.0))
InsulatingTorus{Float64} (center-line radius R=1.0, tube radius r=0.35)

Its concentration tensor ​

The gradient concentration tensor is the mean temperature gradient over the pore per unit remote gradient. It is transversely isotropic about the axis and depends on only:

julia
K₀ = TensISO{3}(2.0)                       # conductivity of the matrix
A = gradient_gradient_concentration(torus, K₀, K₀)
canonical_components(A)
3×3 Tensors.SymmetricTensor{2, 3, Float64, 6}:
 1.43322  0.0      0.0
 0.0      1.43322  0.0
 0.0      0.0      2.04261

The property of the phase, the second argument, is not read: no flux passes through the torus, and its flux concentration is zero. The contributions follow from these two tensors,     and    per unit volume of the torus:

julia
canonical_components(inverse_property_contribution(torus, K₀, K₀))
3×3 Tensors.SymmetricTensor{2, 3, Float64, 6}:
 0.716609  0.0       0.0
 0.0       0.716609  0.0
 0.0       0.0       1.0213

A thin ring has the components across the axis and along it:

julia
diag(canonical_components(gradient_gradient_concentration(InsulatingTorus(1.0, 1.0e-3), K₀, K₀)))
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.4999972234895975
 1.4999972234895975
 2.0000018093007945

In a scheme ​

The torus is a phase like any other, measured by its volume fraction. Aligned pores make the material transversely isotropic:

julia
function porous(pore; kw...)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :matrix, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:K => K₀); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :pores, pore, Dict(:K => K₀); fraction = 0.1, kw...)
    return rve
end
diag(canonical_components(homogenize(porous(torus), MoriTanaka(), :K)))
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.725258778563127
 1.725258778563127
 1.6300499418609848

Randomly oriented pores give an isotropic material:

julia
first(TensND.get_data(homogenize(porous(torus; symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize()), MoriTanaka(), :K)))
1.6923103203990633

The reference medium must be isotropic. A self-consistent or a differential scheme evaluates the pore in its current estimate, which is anisotropic when the pores are aligned: these schemes need symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize() on the phase.

Derivatives ​

The radii are plain numbers of the inclusion, reached by ForwardDiff through the series:

julia
using ForwardDiff
axial(r) = canonical_components(gradient_gradient_concentration(InsulatingTorus(1.0, r), K₀, K₀))[3, 3]
ForwardDiff.derivative(axial, 0.35)
0.15470476521639043

What is not available ​

  • an anisotropic reference medium, refused with a message;

  • a torus that conducts, or whose cross-section is not a circle: a shape given as a region is treated on a grid of Gaussians (Heterogeneities of any shape), within a few percent;

  • the elastic problem.
