Choosing an inclusion ​

Before this page

Concentration and contribution tensors, which defines the tensors a scheme reads from a phase, and The inclusion zoo, which draws the shapes.

A scheme reads one thing from a phase: the response of a single inclusion embedded in the reference medium. This page compares the ways the package obtains that response, so that the route can be chosen from what is known of the morphology and from what is needed downstream: an anisotropic reference medium, a derivative, a given accuracy.

Three levels of entry ​

The response is supplied at one of three levels, and the package derives everything above the level supplied (Custom inclusions gives the functions):

LevelSuppliedExists when
Athe Hill tensor the field inside the inclusion is uniform: an ellipsoid and its limits (The Eshelby inclusion problem)
Bthe two concentration tensors, and always; it is the only level of a heterogeneous inclusion or of a shape that is not an ellipsoid
Cthe contribution tensors and always; it is what a published table of a shape usually gives

Levels A and B serve every scheme. Level C does not give the concentration tensor, so a phase measured by a volume fraction and entered at level C is refused by MoriTanaka and by the self-consistent schemes; a phase measured by a density (a crack) is not concerned.

Each route below ends in the same add_phase! call. They differ by how the response is computed, and therefore by what they allow.

Three consequences of this table.

An isotropic reference medium restricts the iterative schemes. A self-consistent or a differential scheme evaluates the inclusion in its current estimate, which is anisotropic as soon as the inclusions are aligned. A route limited to an isotropic reference then needs symmetrize = IsoSymmetrize() on the phase; the one-shot schemes (Dilute, MoriTanaka, Maxwell) are not concerned.

A derivative with respect to the shape excludes the grid and the mesh. The nodes inside a contour or the elements of a mesh do not vary continuously with a shape parameter, and both routes refuse the request. The closed forms, the layered patterns and the networks accept it (Sensitivities).

An exact route is preferred whenever it exists. A grid, a mesh or a network is for a morphology that has no closed form. The accuracy columns of Heterogeneities of any shape, Finite-element inclusions and The models shipped today measure each of them against an ellipsoid, where the exact answer is known.

A morphology used once in a study is best entered with the callbacks of CustomInclusion. One that is reused, and whose parameters are to be differentiated, deserves a type: the fields of a subtype of AbstractCustomInclusion are reached by derivative with no extra code (Adding a new inclusion).
