Cross-property connections ​

Before this page

Extension to conductivity, where the conductive counterparts of the Hill and contribution tensors are defined, and Crack opening displacement and compliance for the contribution of a crack.

The same pores and cracks lower the stiffness and the conductivity of a material. When both changes are written with the contributions of the inhomogeneities, a measurement of one property tells something of the other. This page states when such a connection is exact, when it is an approximation, and what the approximation drops; the subject is reviewed by Sevostianov and Kachanov (2009) and Kachanov and Sevostianov (2018).

1. Two sums over the same inhomogeneities ​

Without interactions between inhomogeneities, the compliance and the resistivity   of a material are those of the matrix plus one contribution per inhomogeneity,

and (per unit volume of the material) being the compliance and resistivity contributions of Extension to conductivity and of the crack pages. A connection between the two properties exists when each is a function of the corresponding , the same for all , and linear: the sums then go through it.

Parallel inhomogeneities of one shape. All the are proportional to one tensor, all the to another, with the same weight (the volume, or the crack density): every component of   is proportional to every nonzero component of  , and the connection is exact whatever the concentration in that approximation.

Other distributions. Inhomogeneities of several orientations add tensors of different directions, and a linear map from to common to all orientations exists only if depends on the orientation through alone. Cracks show what fails and how little it weighs.

4. Beyond the non-interacting approximation ​

A scheme replaces the sums of §1 by its own estimate, and the connection becomes a relation between two estimates of the same cell, which the package computes by homogenizing one cell that carries both properties :C and :K. Whether the ratio survives the interactions is then a question for each scheme, answered numerically in the application.
