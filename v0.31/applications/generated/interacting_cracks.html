Interacting cracks: the method of average tractions ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/109_interacting_cracks.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/109_interacting_cracks.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Interacting cracks, which derives the method and the fields used here, and Interacting cracks of a plane for the syntax.

Two cracks close to each other change each other's stress intensity factors: collinear cracks amplify the factors at the tips that face each other, stacked cracks shield each other. Kachanov (1987) computes these factors by keeping, in the tractions each crack sends onto the others, only the part due to its mean traction. This page compares the method with the exact solutions of two collinear cracks and of a periodic row of cracks (Tables 1 and 2 of the article), shows how a stack of cracks shields its members in an isotropic and in an orthotropic plane (Mauge and Kachanov, 1994), and ends with an arbitrary array.

A crack of the plane is a RibbonCrack of half-length b whose direction makes the angle angle with , placed at a center; sif takes a list of center => crack pairs, the stiffness of the plane and the remote stress, and returns for each crack its factors at the tips (plus) and (minus) along its direction, and its mean tractions. In an isotropic plane the stresses of a loaded crack do not depend on the moduli: any isotropic stiffness serves.

julia
C_iso = TensISO{2}(2.0, 1.0)
tension = Tens([0.0 0.0; 0.0 1.0])                   ## unit tension normal to e₁

Two collinear cracks ​

The cracks occupy    on the axis  , under the remote tension  . Each has the half-length    and  . The right crack has its inner tip at  , its tip minus, and its outer tip at  :

julia
pair(k) = (
    c = (1 + k) / 2; l = (1 - k) / 2;
    [[-c, 0.0] => RibbonCrack(l; angle = 0.0), [c, 0.0] => RibbonCrack(l; angle = 0.0)]
)
function collinear(k)
    right = sif(pair(k), C_iso, tension)[2]
    K₀ = sqrt(π * (1 - k) / 2)
    return (inner = right.minus[1] / K₀, outer = right.plus[1] / K₀)
end

The exact solution, derived in Interacting cracks, §4, from Westergaard's function of the two cracks: with   and   , the factors at the outer and inner tips are   and   times .

julia
function collinear_exact(k)
    λ² = ell_E(1 - k^2) / ell_K(1 - k^2)
    f = sqrt(2 / (1 - k)) / sqrt(1 - k^2)
    return (inner = (λ² - k^2) / sqrt(k) * f, outer = (1 - λ²) * f)
end

The closed form of the method. For two collinear cracks the method has the closed form (6) of the article: each crack transmits to the other the mean traction , with     (its eq. 4), so that   , and the factors follow from eq. (14) applied to the traction of eq. (3):

julia
function collinear_eq6(k)
    Λ = sqrt(2(1 + k)) / (1 + sqrt(k)) - 1
    K, E = ell_K(1 - k^2), ell_E(1 - k^2)
    a = 1 / ((1 - Λ) * π * (1 - k))
    return (inner = 1 + a * (-2E + (k + 1) * K - π / 2 * (1 - k)), outer = 1 + a * (2E - k * (k + 1) * K - π / 2 * (1 - k)))
end

The method gives back its printed column to the last digit, and the closed form the exact column. The method underestimates the factor at the inner tips, by 10 per cent when the gap is a fiftieth of the length of a crack: the traction a crack sends onto its neighbor is concentrated near the tip that faces it, and its mean misses that.

julia
gaps = exp10.(range(-2.3, 0.5; length = 60))          ## 2k/(1 - k), gap over length
k_of(g) = g / (2 + g)
plot(
    gaps, [getfield.(collinear.(k_of.(gaps)), :inner) getfield.(collinear_exact.(k_of.(gaps)), :inner)];
    xscale = :log10, lw = 2, ls = [:solid :dash], color = [1 1],
    label = ["inner tip, average tractions" "inner tip, exact"],
    xlabel = "gap / crack length", ylabel = "K_I / K_I⁰", size = (700, 420)
)
plot!(
    gaps, [getfield.(collinear.(k_of.(gaps)), :outer) getfield.(collinear_exact.(k_of.(gaps)), :outer)];

function Λ(j, ζ)
    u = 1 / (j * ζ)
    s = sqrt(1 + u) + sqrt(1 - u)
    return 2u^2 / (s^2 * (1 + sqrt(1 + u)) * (1 + sqrt(1 - u)))

Stacked cracks shield each other ​

Two parallel cracks of half-length  , one above the other at the distance , under the tension normal to them: each lowers the factor of the other. In an anisotropic plane the field of a loaded crack is that of the slit of the material, which sif uses when given an anisotropic stiffness. The orthotropic plane below, given by its Kelvin–Mandel matrix, has   and  : it is stiffer along the cracks; the second one, turned by 90°, is stiffer across them.

julia

The factor tends to 1 as the cracks move apart. The shielding depends on the anisotropy: it is weaker in the plane stiffer along the cracks, stronger in the one stiffer across them. Collinear cracks do not see it: the stress a loaded slit creates on its own line, ahead of its tips, is the same in every material (Mauge and Kachanov, 1994):
