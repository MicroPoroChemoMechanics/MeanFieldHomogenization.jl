Fluid-filled pores: pressure polarization ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/108_fluid_filled_pores.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/108_fluid_filled_pores.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Levin's theorem, §5, which defines the pressure of a fluid sealed in a pore family and derives Skempton's coefficient of a sphere, and Poromechanics, which gives the syntax of pore_pressure_tensor.

A fluid trapped in a pore of a loaded solid takes a pressure that depends on the shape of the pore and on its orientation with respect to the load: two pores of the same rock, filled with the same fluid, hold different pressures. Shafiro and Kachanov call this the pressure polarization and give it in closed form for a sphere, a crack and a needle. This page computes the pressure for spheroids of any aspect ratio, against their formulas, then passes to a finite porosity, where sealed and connected pores part.

The fluid is given by its compressibility kappa (MPa^-1), the matrix by E0 (MPa) and nu0. The pressure q in a cavity is q = Q : sigma, eq. (6), positive in compression, sigma being the remote stress (tension positive). The compressibility of the dry cavity is C_p = R/E0, that of the matrix C0 = 3(1 - 2 nu0)/E0, and delta = (kappa - C0)/C_p, eq. (8). The aspect ratio is zeta = a3/a, a3 the semi-axis along the axis of symmetry n of the spheroid. The pressure tensor of one pore, of shape shape, in a solid of Young modulus E and Poisson ratio ν, filled with a fluid of compressibility κ; and the compressibility of the same pore dry, its volume strain per unit confining pressure, read on its strain concentration tensor (gradient_flux_concentration):

solid(E, ν) = TensISO{3}(E / (1 - 2ν), E / (1 + ν))                ## 3k 𝕁 + 2μ 𝕂
const 𝟏 = TensISO{3}(1.0)
function lone_pore(shape, κ; E = E₀, ν = ν₀)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :solid, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => solid(E, ν)); fraction = :rest)

end

The sphere: Skempton's coefficient ​

The volume of a pore filled with fluid changes as the fluid it holds. The change of volume of the pore is that of the dry pore under the remote pressure, , plus that of the pore under the fluid pressure in an unloaded solid,   , where    is the compressibility of the solid (the second term is the uniform state   plus the dry pore under the remote stress  ). The fluid changes its volume by  , whence eq. (14) of the article,

valid for any shape of pore, the shape entering through alone. For the sphere,    (   , eq. (10)), and the coefficient is that of Eshelby's solution,    (Levin's theorem, §5). The three against the Poisson ratio of the solid, for water and for fluids ten times more and ten times less compressible:

julia
eq14(C_p, κ, E, ν) = C_p / (C_p + κ - 3(1 - 2ν) / E)
function eshelby(κ, E, ν)
    k, μ = E / (3(1 - 2ν)), E / (2(1 + ν))
    return (3k + 4μ) / (k * (3 + 4μ * κ))                        ## k_f(3k + 4μ)/(k(3k_f + 4μ)), k_f = 1/κ
end
sphere = Ellipsoid(1.0)
νs = range(0, 0.45; length = 46)
κs = (0.1κ_w, κ_w, 10κ_w)
B = [skempton(sphere, κ; ν) for ν in νs, κ in κs]
gap_14 = maximum(abs(B[i, j] - eq14(dry_compressibility(sphere; ν), κ, E₀, ν)) for (i, ν) in pairs(νs), (j, κ) in pairs(κs))
gap_eshelby = maximum(abs(B[i, j] - eshelby(κ, E₀, ν)) for (i, ν) in pairs(νs), (j, κ) in pairs(κs))
gap_R = maximum(abs(E₀ * dry_compressibility(sphere; ν) - 9(1 - ν) / 2) for ν in νs)
(eq_14 = gap_14, eshelby = gap_eshelby, R = gap_R)
(eq_14 = 3.3306690738754696e-16, eshelby = 3.3306690738754696e-16, R = 3.9968028886505635e-15)

A finite porosity: sealed or connected pores ​

At a finite porosity the pores interact, and MoriTanaka replaces the dilute scheme. When the pores are connected, one pressure reigns in all of them and the undrained stiffness follows from the drained one by Gassmann's relation, in its anisotropic form of Brown and Korringa (poroelastic_parameters with k_f, then undrained_stiffness). When they are sealed, each family keeps its pressure. A cell of granite with pores of given shapes, either filled with water or dry:

julia
const C₀ = solid(E₀, ν₀)
function granite(families; κ = κ_w)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :solid, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C₀); fraction = :rest)
    for (name, shape, f) in families
        add_phase!(rve, name, shape, Dict(:C => TensISO{3}(3 / κ, 0.0)); fraction = f)
    end
    return rve
end
porosity(families) = sum(last, families)
function sealed_and_connected(families)
    C_sealed = homogenize(granite(families), MoriTanaka())
    C_dry = homogenize(granite(families; κ = Inf), MoriTanaka())
    p = poroelastic_parameters(C_dry, C₀, porosity(families); k_f = 1 / κ_w)
    C_connected = undrained_stiffness(C_dry, p.B, p.modulus)
    return bulk_shear(best_fit_iso(C_sealed)), bulk_shear(best_fit_iso(C_connected))
end

Sealed, the cracks keep their water, which stiffens them; connected, the water of a compressed crack escapes to the spheres and to the cracks that the load opens. The pressures of the sealed families under a confining pressure, by Mori-Tanaka, show the imbalance that drives the flow: the compliant cracks squeeze their water to about the confining pressure (a little more, the solid around them carrying more than the remote stress), the stiff spheres shield theirs.
