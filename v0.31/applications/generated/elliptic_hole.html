The elliptic hole, from an isotropic to an anisotropic plate ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/90_elliptic_hole.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/90_elliptic_hole.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Holes given by a conformal map and the appendices Complex potentials and The elliptic hole in an anisotropic matrix, which derive what is computed here, and Conformal holes, which gives the syntax.

A plate pulled in its plane carries, along the edge of an elliptic hole, a stress several times the applied one. This page computes that stress in an isotropic plate, against the closed form of Muskhelishvili, then in an orthotropic plywood sheet, against the formulas and the tables of Lekhnitskii, follows the stress away from the edge, and ends with the same hole as an inclusion of a homogenized sheet and as an insulator.

julia
muskhelishvili(m, α, θ) = (1 - m^2 + 2m * cos(2α) - 2cos(2(θ - α))) / (1 - 2m * cos(2θ) + m^2)
const ratios = (1, 2, 3, 5)
println("a/b   stress at the tip   1 + 2a/b   largest deviation from the closed form")
for c in ratios
    m = (c - 1) / (c + 1)
    σ = contour_stress(ellipse(c), across, θs)
    @printf "%d     %10.6f        %6.3f     %.1e\n" c first(σ) 1 + 2c maximum(abs, σ - muskhelishvili.(m, π / 2, θs))
end

Read in Table 6, p. 64; Section 31, Eqs. (31.1), (31.17) and (31.18), Tables 8, 9 and 10, pp. 170-175 checked against the source

Transcribed from the book (scanned PDF), read on the images of the pages

An infinite plate with an elliptic hole of semi-axes a >= b, pulled by a uniform stress p at infinity, in generalized plane stress. phi is the angle of the pull with the major axis of the ellipse, psi the angle of the principal direction of elasticity of the larger Young modulus with that axis, both counted in the same sense (Fig. 46 of the book). The tables give the largest tensile stress and the largest compressive stress (as a positive number) along the edge of the hole, divided by p, for the ratio a/b = 2 and the three-ply birch plywood of Table 6; the book says that two decimals are kept. The moduli of Table 6 are printed as multiples of 10^5 kgf/cm^2 and are stored as these multiples: only their ratios enter.

julia
function plywood(E₁, E₂, G, ν₁, frame)
    ν₂ = ν₁ * E₂ / E₁                                          ## the symmetry of the compliance
    d = 1 - ν₁ * ν₂
    return TensOrtho(E₁ / d, E₂ / d, ν₁ * E₂ / d, G, frame)    ## C₁₁₁₁, C₂₂₂₂, C₁₁₂₂, C₁₂₁₂ in the frame

Away from the edge ​
