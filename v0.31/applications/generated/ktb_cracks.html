Cracks of the KTB gneiss read on its velocities ​

The whole page as one script

Every block of this page, in order, is scripts/107_ktb_crack_characterization.jl. From a clone of the repository, julia --project=docs -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia scripts/107_ktb_crack_characterization.jl, runs it end to end.

Before this page

Cracks seen as fractures, sections 2 and 3, which define the compliances of a set of fractures and invert three orthogonal sets, Seismic signatures for the syntax, and the Plane waves of TensND.

A gneiss cored in the pilot hole of the German deep drilling program (KTB) was measured by ultrasonic waves at two confining pressures, 400 MPa, where its cracks are closed, and 10 MPa, where they are open (Rasolofosaon et al., 2000). The difference of the two compliances is the compliance of the cracks. This page reads on it the frame of the cracks, the normal and tangential compliances of three orthogonal sets of fractures, and what their ratio says about the cracks, and rebuilds the two models of the article.

julia
using MeanFieldHomogenization
using TensND
using LinearAlgebra
using JSON
using Printf
using Plots
gr()

The two measured stiffnesses ​

The article prints the two stiffness matrices in the two-index (Voigt) notation, in GPa, in the frame of the sample, vertical (its Figs 5a and 5b). stiffness_from_voigt turns each into a tensor ; voigt_compliance writes a compliance back in the same notation, with   . Compliances are printed in  , that is  .

julia
lit = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(MeanFieldHomogenization), "data", "literature", "rasolofosaonGJI2000.json"))
voigt(name) = [lit["tables"][name][i]["c$j"] for i in 1:6, j in 1:6]       ## GPa
axes_of(name) = [lit["tables"][name][j][c] for c in ("x", "y", "z"), j in 1:3]   ## one axis per column
C_400 = stiffness_from_voigt(voigt("stiffness_400MPa"))
C_10 = stiffness_from_voigt(voigt("stiffness_10MPa"))
ρ = lit["quantities"]["density"]["value"] / 1000                            ## g/cm³: velocities in km/s
per_TPa(S) = round.(voigt_compliance(S) * 1.0e3; digits = 3)                ## 1e-12 Pa⁻¹

Rasolofosaon, Patrick N. J., Rabbel, Wolfgang, Siegesmund, Siegfried, Vollbrecht, Axel (2000). Characterization of crack distribution: fabric analysis versus ultrasonic inversion. Geophysical Journal International 141(2), 413-424. doi:10.1046/j.1365-246x.2000.00093.x

Read in Figs 5 and 6 (pp. 419-420), text of pp. 418 and 420-422 (eqs 1-7) checked against the source

Transcribed from the published article (PDF), on pages rendered at 300 dpi; every value compared with the page image

Paragneiss sample 767 G3y of the KTB pilot hole (depth 3145 m). The stiffness matrices are in the two-index (Voigt) notation, GPa, in the sample frame (X, Y, Z), Z vertical; the figures print the upper triangle, stored here in full. The excess compliance and the fracture compliances are in the two-index notation of compliances (S44 = 4 S2323), in the crack frame (Xc, Yc, Zc), 1e-12 Pa^-1. The crack frame was found by the authors as the best orthotropic frame of the cracked medium (programs of Arts et al. 1991 and Arts 1993, not described in the article). The frame printed is left-handed (determinant -1), which does not change a fourth-order tensor. The deviations from higher symmetries are the boxes 'Deviations from higher symmetries' of Figs 5 and 6, in per cent; the article defines them by reference to Arts et al. (1991) without giving the formula.

The excess compliance in the frame of the cracks ​

The components of in the frame are those of the tensor expressed in the basis of its axes (express). The printed frame is rounded to three decimals and is left-handed: it is made orthonormal by its polar factor and right-handed by turning one axis, which changes no component of a tensor of order four.

function orthonormal(A)                              ## polar factor, made right-handed
    f = svd(A)
    Q = f.U * f.Vt
    return Q * Diagonal([1, 1, sign(det(Q))])

What the ratio of the compliances says ​

The ratio   does not depend on the density of cracks: it depends on their shape and on what fills them. For dry flat cracks, isolated in an isotropic rock, the stress-driven dilute scheme gives the compliances of a family of penny-shaped cracks of density (number per unit volume times the cube of the radius) (Kachanov, 1992),

fracture_compliance reads them on a cell, here the isotropic intact rock of the article with one family of unit density:

julia
function dry_cracks(C)
    rve = RVE()
    add_phase!(rve, :rock, Ellipsoid(1.0), Dict(:C => C); fraction = :rest)
    add_phase!(rve, :cracks, PennyCrack(1.0), Dict(:C => C); density = 1.0)
    return fracture_compliance(rve, DiluteDual(), :cracks)
end
dry = dry_cracks(C_iso)
E, ν = young_modulus(C_iso), poisson_ratio(C_iso)
